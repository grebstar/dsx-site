SCOUTING REPORT

Brooklyn took him #27 overall out of Utah. He had just gone for 16.4 points, 9.3 rebounds and 2.4 assists in 29 games at 30.8 minutes a night. He went 9 picks ahead of his composite, which was #36. The boards ranged from #29 to #60 on him, a spread of 31 places across 4 of them.

2017-18: 77 games, 37 starts, 31.2 minutes — 16.1 / 6.3 / 1.8 on 45.0% shooting. That ranked him third in the class and marked him at $623.16, +4612.1% against his IPO. 2025-26: 69 games, 43 starts, 26.2 minutes — 13.0 / 4.5 / 2.7 on 49.2% shooting. That ranked him twelfth in the class and marked him at $131.64, +895.4% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $13.22, today's mark of $131.64 is +895.4%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished third in the All-Rookie vote among 2017 draftees.

Brooklyn drafted him twenty-seventh and traded him, with Brook Lopez, to the Lakers that same night for D'Angelo Russell and Timofey Mozgov — he was never a Net in any functional sense. He was part of the Lakers' 2020 NBA championship roster in his third season. On Aug. 6, 2021, the Lakers traded him, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, Montrezl Harrell and that year's No. 22 pick to Washington for Russell Westbrook; he signed a 4-year, $102 million extension with Washington in Jul. 2023. On Feb. 5-6, 2025, a four-team trade sent him to Milwaukee, with Washington receiving Khris Middleton, AJ Johnson and a 2028 first-round pick swap.
