SCOUTING REPORT

Coulibaly is the purest tools bet in the lottery. He averaged 6.1 points in 20.2 minutes as Wembanyama's teammate at Metropolitans 92, which is not a statistical case for anything — and Washington still gave up the 8th pick and two second-rounders to move up one spot for him.

What they paid for is a 6'8" wing with a reported 7'2" wingspan who turned 19 the month after the draft, moves like a guard, and shot 52.6% from the field with 0.9 steals in limited minutes. RookieScale and Fantrax had him in the low teens — RookieScale 11th, Fantrax 13th — which is roughly where a category league prices frame and age when production is absent.

The bear case is simply the line. 6.1 points, 1.0 assists, 0.2 blocks and 60.9% from the free-throw line: there is nothing here that wins a category in year one or two. Washington won 35 games and had a completely open depth chart, so he will play immediately — which is the only reason to hold him early. This is a three-year hold priced in the top twelve on faith.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 6'8" with an enormous wingspan, turning 19 a month after the draft
  • 52.6% from the field and 0.9 steals in a professional league as a teenager
  • Washington surrendered a pick and two seconds to move up for him
  • The emptiest depth chart in the league guarantees immediate minutes

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 6.1 points a game — there is essentially no statistical case
  • 60.9% from the line and 1.0 assists; multiple dead columns
  • 18.2% from three across four Summer League games
  • Washington will be historically bad, which caps everything but usage
