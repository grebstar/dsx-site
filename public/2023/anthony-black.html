SCOUTING REPORT

Black is 6'7" and plays point guard, and the Arkansas line shows why that matters: 2.1 steals alongside 3.9 assists and 5.1 rebounds. Guard-sized playmaking with forward-sized rebounding and elite steal volume is a genuinely useful category shape, and RookieScale ranked him 6th on it.

Fantrax ranked him 11th, and the five-place gap is the shooting. 30.1% from three on 0.8 makes and 70.5% from the line is a hole that shrinks everything else — a non-shooting lead guard gets played off the floor in the modern NBA, however big he is. 3.0 turnovers against 3.9 assists does not help.

The bigger constraint is Orlando. The Magic were an ascending team with Paolo Banchero, Franz Wagner, Jalen Suggs and Markelle Fultz already occupying the ball-handling minutes; they went on to win 47 games in his rookie year. There was no year-one path, and Pathway 62 against a model base of 90 reflects it. Priced 8th — a talent buy with a two-year wait attached.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 2.1 steals from a 6'7" lead guard — the scarcest category at an unusual position
  • 5.1 rebounds and 3.9 assists; three columns filled before the scoring is counted
  • RookieScale ranked him 6th, exactly at his draft slot
  • Orlando's development infrastructure and defensive culture suit the profile

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 30.1% from three and 70.5% from the line — a non-shooting guard in a spacing league
  • 3.0 turnovers against 3.9 assists
  • Orlando's backcourt was full and the team was trying to win now
  • 16.7% from three across three Summer League games
