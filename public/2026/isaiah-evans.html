SCOUTING REPORT

Start with the shooting, because it is the whole case. 15.0 points, 3.2 rebounds, 1.3 assists, 0.7 steals and 2.7 made threes in 28.2 minutes on 43.3% from the field, 36.1% from three and 86.0% from the line across 38 games at Duke, every one of them a start. He led the Blue Devils with 101 made threes on 7.4 attempts a game, scored 20-plus nine times, and was named All-ACC Third Team and ACC All-Tournament First Team. The 86.0% at the line says the touch is real rather than a volume artifact.

The frame is the hedge. He measured 6'5.5" barefoot at 186 pounds with a 6'8.75" wingspan at the combine. The length is genuine for a wing; the weight is not. Draft analysts consistently flagged the thin frame and trouble finishing through contact, and Tankathon notes a low assist-to-usage ratio — he is a play-finisher and spacer, not a creator. His 1.3 assists against 1.1 turnovers is the playmaking ceiling in one line.

Brooklyn selected him 33rd and Minnesota holds his rights; he signed July 12 on a standard four-year, $9.3 million contract with three years guaranteed and a team option on the fourth. Reports differ on the exact structure of the trade, so we do not describe it. Summer League was rough: 4 points on 2-of-15 against Denver, then 22 points on 9-of-22 against Indiana, and 8-for-39 from three across the event.

The role is crowded. Minnesota's projected starters include Edwards, Ball, McDaniels, Kuminga and Gobert, and SI's Ben Beecken wrote that Evans is unlikely to be relied on this season. The opening is Donte DiVincenzo's ruptured Achilles, which leaves wing shooting minutes available.

How he is ranked. Evans is the one profile on this board priced from the Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards alone. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 28th, 24th and 24th in this class. Angel, Dizzle and Roto carry him only as a range, not as individual ranks, so they do not set his number.

The read. At $33.31 and 26th, he is a first-round shooting grade at a second-round slot, with a tight four-spot spread across the three boards that ranked him. The cost is the nine-category format: with a 9-Cat EPS of −2.12 he gives you threes and free throw percentage and little else. The Summer League slump is noise next to 38 games of college shooting, but the roster says patience.
