SCOUTING REPORT

Before the injury, this was one of the best storylines in the entire draft class. Saunders broke out as a junior, winning Big 12 Most Improved Player and First-Team All-Big 12 honors, then kept climbing as a senior — 18.0 points, 5.8 rebounds, 2.1 assists and 1.7 steals on a 48.9/37.6/81.7 shooting line before a torn ACL in the opening minute against Colorado ended his season. Earlier that year, in BYU's run to its first Sweet 16 in decades, he'd posted 22.0 points and 5.7 rebounds while shooting an eye-popping 59.5/41.7/100 across the tournament run — becoming the fifth player in program history to hit 50/40/80 for a full season along the way.

Memphis took him at 32, reportedly fielding "a ton of trade interest" league-wide for the pick and choosing to stand pat specifically to get him — joining Cameron Boozer and Bennett Stirtz as the third Grizzlies rookie from this class. The 1.7 steals a game from a 6'5" wing is a real, translatable defensive trait layered on top of legitimate shot-making.

The injury is the entire near-term story. He's not expected to be available for the start of the 2026-27 season, and he is already the oldest player in this seven-player group at nearly 25. A torn ACL at that age, on a player whose value is built on movement and quickness rather than pure size, is a real and unresolved variable — not every recovery returns a player to his pre-injury explosiveness.

The read. The talent case was never in dispute; he was one of the best shooters in the country before the injury. The entire value of this pick is a bet on a full recovery, on a player who doesn't have size or age working in his favor if it doesn't fully return.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 27th, 45th and 38th in this class. Yahoo is the high mark at 27th and Sporting News the low at 45th.
