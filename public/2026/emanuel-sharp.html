SCOUTING REPORT

Four years as a full-time starter at Houston under Kelvin Sampson — a program built entirely around toughness and defensive principles — produced a senior season of 15.5 points, 3.0 rebounds, 1.7 assists and 1.2 steals on 41.3% shooting, 37.2% from three on real volume, and an outstanding 89.1% from the free throw line. He earned First-Team All-Big 12 and All-Defensive Team honors in the same season, and Houston's defense allowed the fewest points per game in the conference with him as a primary perimeter stopper.

Sacramento took him at 45, the third of three picks in a draft clearly built around adding perimeter shooting to a roster that finished near the bottom of the league in three-point percentage. The front office was explicit that cost-controlled, experienced role players were the target with this class, and Sharp — alongside Acuff and Karaban — fits that mandate directly.

The limiter is size and shot creation, and it's a hard ceiling. At 6'2.75" with a 6'3" wingspan — a negative wingspan-to-height ratio, unusual even for a small guard — he isn't a self-creator and doesn't project as one. One evaluator's honest read: "too small" with skill level "outside of shooting" that doesn't do enough to earn a first-round grade. He is expected to open the year with Sacramento's G League affiliate, both to develop and because the Kings' backcourt is already crowded with Acuff and Karaban.

The read. This is a specialist bet on a specific NBA skill — knockdown shooting paired with legitimate defensive toughness — landing in a crowded depth chart. The talent for the role is real and proven over four years; the immediate opportunity is not.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 41st, 44th and 39th in this class.
