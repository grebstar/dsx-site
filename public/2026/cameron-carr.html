SCOUTING REPORT

The college line is better than his draft slot suggests. 18.9 points, 5.8 rebounds, 2.6 assists, 1.3 blocks and 2.3 made threes in 33.7 minutes on 49.4% from the field and 80.1% from the line at Baylor. His nine-cat composite of +1.77 ranks nineteenth in this class. The blocks are the interesting part — wings who protect the rim at 1.3 a game while making better than two threes are a genuinely scarce archetype, and it is exactly the profile that ages well.

New York took him at 24 and traded him to the Lakers. The fit reasoning is straightforward and was laid out plainly by post-draft analysis: with LeBron gone, Los Angeles spent the offseason adding wing depth, and Carr's ability to space, defend and finish above the rim slots cleanly next to Doncic and Reaves. That is a real role with a real path, just not an immediate one.

The bear case is usage. Complementary wings on star-driven offenses get 8–11 shot attempts, and 18.9 points at Baylor will not travel. What travels is the block rate, the three-point volume and the defensive activity — but 0.9 steals is unremarkable and he needs the threes to hold up at NBA distance. He is also 21.7, older than most of the prospects ranked near him.

The read. At $56.94 he sits twentieth. Angel had him fifteenth, the most bullish read on the board, while Roto and Dizzle had him eighteenth. He is a reasonable late-first stash on a stable, well-run platform, with a category profile that fits a nine-cat roster better than his scoring line implies. Nothing about him is likely to disappoint; nothing about him is likely to excite either.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 23rd, 19th and 20th in this class.
