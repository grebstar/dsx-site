SCOUTING REPORT

The skills are real and worth stating. 66.8% from the field is the highest mark on this entire board, and 2.6 blocks in 23.4 minutes is elite rim protection at any level. He also passes — 2.4 assists from a center is unusual and speaks to genuine feel. At 21.3 he is young enough to develop, and Oklahoma City is the best development organization in the league.

But the nine-category math is unforgiving. Points at 12.1, rebounds at 6.8, and then: 0.1 made threes, 0.4 steals, 1.2 assists, 2.0 turnovers and 56.4% from the line. That is two elite categories, two neutral ones and five that range from useless to actively harmful. His overall nine-cat composite of +0.32 ranks below players fifteen spots beneath him on this board, and that gap is the format penalty for narrow production.

The opportunity picture is the worst in the lottery. Chet Holmgren, Isaiah Hartenstein and Jaylin Williams are all ahead of him and all under contract. His Pathway to Early Minutes score of 46 is the lowest of any lottery pick on the board by a wide margin. He also struggled visibly at Summer League, which was widely noted.

The read. At $170.70, tenth on the board, he is priced for a category-specialist role that most rosters cannot afford to carry. If you are punting free throw percentage and desperately need blocks, he is a defensible target. On a roster that is trying to win field goal percentage and free throw percentage, he is a structural mismatch — the FT% loss cancels the FG% gain and you are paying a first-round rookie pick for blocks alone. SELL is a rating on the fit, not on the player.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 8th, 16th and 14th in this class. Yahoo is the high mark at 8th and Sporting News the low at 16th.
