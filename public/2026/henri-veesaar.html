SCOUTING REPORT

Start with what the box score says, because it's remarkable for the position. 17.0 points, 8.7 rebounds, 2.1 assists and 1.2 blocks on 60.8% from the field and 42.7% from three (on 94 attempts — real volume, not a hot streak), with 61 total made threes. A 6'11" center shooting over 60% inside the arc and over 42% outside it is one of the rarest efficiency combinations in the sport, and it nearly doubled his previous season's scoring output after transferring from Arizona to North Carolina.

He was widely considered a late-first-round talent for most of the pre-draft process. The fall to 52 was explicitly called a surprise by outlets covering the draft, and the mechanism behind it is telling: Atlanta had to trade up from the No. 57 pick specifically to jump ahead of the Knicks and Lakers, both reportedly interested at the same range. That's a fast, decisive move by a front office that saw value the broader board had mispriced.

The two real weaknesses are consistent across every scouting report. 61.5% free throw shooting that has declined every season as his attempts increased is a legitimate red flag — NBA defenses will send him to the line late in games specifically to exploit it. And at a slim 227 pounds for his height, he needs real strength added before he can hold up physically against NBA centers; he graded poorly defending in space against ball-handlers who could put the ball on the floor.

The read. The shooting profile alone — genuine size combined with genuine range — is a skill set that is increasingly scarce and increasingly valuable around the league. At a 52nd-pick price, with a front office that already showed conviction by trading up for him, this is one of the better value plays in the entire class.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 26th, 30th and 23rd in this class. CBS is the high mark at 23rd and Sporting News the low at 30th.
