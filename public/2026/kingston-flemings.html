SCOUTING REPORT

The line is quietly excellent. 16.1 points, 5.2 assists, 4.1 rebounds and 1.5 steals in 31.7 minutes on 47.6% from the field and 84.5% from the line, with only 1.8 turnovers. Five categories at or above the class median, no category actively bleeding, and the efficiency is real rather than usage-suppressed. Atlanta took him eighth overall.

What makes him interesting relative to the guards ranked near him is that he does not need volume to be useful. Brown and Acuff require touches to produce; Flemings produces on the touches he gets. Steals and assists are the two categories most correlated with playing time rather than usage, and both are his strengths. The 84.5% free throw rate is the best mark in the top fifteen and is the sort of quiet edge that decides close nine-cat weeks.

The bear case is one word: size. Multiple evaluators have flagged that he is small for a lottery selection, and the development path runs through adding weight. Undersized guards who can't finish through contact see their efficiency compress at the NBA level, and the 1.1 made threes per game means he does not yet have the pull-up volume to compensate. Atlanta's backcourt is not empty either.

The read. At $229.10 he is the eighth listing on the board, and the price feels right rather than cheap. He is one of the safer bets in the 5–10 range precisely because his value doesn't require a leap — it requires him to keep doing what he already does in more minutes. That is a lower-variance path to a top-100 dynasty asset than anything Acuff or Wagler offers.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 10th, 5th and 6th in this class.
