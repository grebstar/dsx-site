SCOUTING REPORT

The opportunity is close to the best in the class. Brooklyn selected him at six and has an open backcourt; he will get the ball on night one and keep it. His Pathway to Early Minutes score of 87 is the second highest on this board, behind only Boozer's. In a redraft context that alone makes him rosterable.

The dynasty math is harder. 18.2 points on 41.0% shooting with 3.1 turnovers in 21 games. The 2.6 made threes and 84.4% free throw shooting are genuine positives — he can shoot when the shot is clean — and 4.7 assists is real creation. But the field goal percentage and the turnovers are two of the nine categories running underwater simultaneously, and both are the kind of problem that volume makes worse rather than better. He also played only 21 games, the second-smallest sample in the top ten.

The team context deserves weight. There is a meaningful difference between a guard putting up 18 and 5 on a playoff team and one doing it on a 20-win roster, and it is not just aesthetic — usage inflated by a lack of alternatives tends to compress the moment real talent arrives. Brooklyn holds significant future draft capital and will keep adding to this roster. The touches he gets in 2026-27 are the most he may ever get.

The read. At $269.91 he is the seventh listing on the board and reasonably valued for what he is: an immediate producer with a genuine efficiency problem and a capped platform. He was solid in Vegas without separating himself. HOLD, with the note that his redraft value in Year 1 will likely exceed his dynasty value — which makes him a sell-into-strength candidate more than a hold-forever asset.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 5th, 14th and 8th in this class. Yahoo is the high mark at 5th and Sporting News the low at 14th.
