SCOUTING REPORT

The talent evaluation is close to a lock. Peterson went second overall to Utah and was the top prospect on several draft boards outright. The nine-cat profile is unusually good for a guard: 20.2 points, 2.6 made threes, 4.2 rebounds, 1.4 steals and 0.6 blocks on 82.6% from the line. Guard rebounding and guard blocks are the two scarcest sources of category surplus in this format, and he supplies both. He was also, by his own coaches' accounting, a considerably better playmaker in high school in a higher-volume role when he was healthy — the 1.6 assists at Kansas reads as an artifact of the injuries, not a fixed trait.

Which brings us to the hole in the balance sheet. Twenty-four games. That is the number. It is the lowest games-played figure of any top-eight prospect on this board, and it is the reason he sits second rather than first on most fantasy-specific rankings despite being first on several talent rankings. Availability is a skill in a head-to-head format, and he has not yet demonstrated it.

The second constraint is distribution. Utah is a crowded, competitive roster, and post-Summer-League commentary has been direct about it — he will face major competition for touches. That is why his Pathway score sits below where a second overall pick's normally would. He was solid but inefficient in Vegas, and the playmaking looked better than advertised, which is the encouraging part.

The read. At $808.24 you are paying a near-top-of-market price for a player whose two open questions are health and touches. The upside case — a 20-point guard with threes, steals and rebounds who plays 75 games — is a top-25 dynasty asset within three years. The downside case is a 55-game season that torches your head-to-head weeks. Both are live.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 2nd, 1st and 2nd in this class.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Several evaluators graded him the best pure prospect in the entire class
  • Rare nine-cat guard shape: 2.6 threes, 4.2 rebounds, 1.4 steals, 0.6 blocks, 82.6% FT
  • The low assist number looks injury-driven; he was a high-volume creator when healthy
  • Went second overall — Utah paid up and will give him developmental runway

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Played only 24 games as a freshman; availability is the entire thesis
  • Utah's guard depth means major competition for touches in Year 1
  • Second-highest offer price on the board leaves no margin of safety
  • Was inefficient from the field in Las Vegas
