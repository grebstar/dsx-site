SCOUTING REPORT

The defensive profile is the reason anyone is still interested. 2.4 blocks and 7.5 rebounds in 27.7 minutes on 52.9% shooting at 19.0 years old — the youngest player on this entire board. Blocks are the second-scarcest category in nine-cat basketball behind steals, and a 19-year-old producing 2.4 of them is a genuinely rare asset. San Antonio spent the twentieth pick on it, which tells you the medical information wasn't disqualifying to at least one front office.

Everything else is a problem. 8.8 points is the lowest scoring output of any player in the top twenty-five. He made 0.2 threes. He turned it over 1.9 times in 27.7 minutes. And the free throw shooting — 45.2% — is genuinely disqualifying for many roster constructions. If you are trying to win free throw percentage, you cannot own him at any price. His nine-cat composite of −1.34 reflects a profile that is elite in one column and negative in four.

The timing risk compounds it. Reports around the draft suggested he may not be ready for training camp, and his Pathway score of 27 reflects both the medical situation and the fact that San Antonio's frontcourt is headlined by Wembanyama. The most likely 2026-27 outcome is very few minutes.

The read. At $39.70, twenty-fourth on the board. All six boards list him, and Angel, Dizzle and Roto have him no higher than twenty-fourth, but CBS's Adam Finkelstein has him thirteenth in this class — the most bullish read on him anywhere. The upside is a legitimate top-60 dynasty asset in four years if the body cooperates and the free throw shooting improves. Both of those have to happen. If you punt free throw percentage, he's a defensible swing; on a roster targeting both percentage categories, he is a structural mismatch regardless of talent.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 37th, 27th and 13th in this class. CBS is the high mark at 13th and Yahoo the low at 37th.
