SCOUTING REPORT

The pre-existing business is worth stating plainly, because it is the reason this is a five-star rating and not a lottery ticket. At North Carolina, Wilson put up 19.8 points, 9.4 rebounds, 2.7 assists, 1.5 steals and 1.4 blocks on 57.8% shooting. In nine-category terms, he is above the class median in six categories and elite in three. A big man who produces both steals and blocks at that rate is the single most valuable archetype in this format, because it is the one you cannot assemble from replacement-level parts.

Then Las Vegas. Thirty-five points on 7-of-11 from three in his debut, one of the great Summer League openers on record, and he kept going — 21 points and 8 rebounds in three quarters a few games later, earning standout honors after every appearance. Coverage of the event converged on the same conclusion: he was the best player in the class in Vegas, and he outplayed Boozer head-to-head. The Bulls took him fourth and it now looks like the value pick of the lottery.

The bear case is the free throw line, and it is real. He shot 71.3% from the stripe in college and roughly 33% at Summer League. In a nine-cat league that is a live liability, and at his likely volume it is not a rounding error. There is also a genuine question of whether eleven three-point attempts across a handful of exhibition games is signal or noise — 0.3 makes per game over a full college season is the larger sample, and it says something different.

The read. At $495.29 he is the fourth listing on the board on a board that has not fully caught up to what happened in July. The composite's own commentary allowed that a case could be made to take him over Peterson or Dybantsa even before Vegas; after Vegas, that case is considerably stronger. This is the one name on the board where the model and the market are furthest apart, and the model is behind.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 4th, 3rd and 4th in this class.
