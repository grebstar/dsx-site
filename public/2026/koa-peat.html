SCOUTING REPORT

The efficient part is real. 14.1 points and 5.6 rebounds on 52.8% shooting in 27.8 minutes at Arizona, with 2.6 assists and only 1.6 turnovers. He finishes inside, he passes acceptably for a forward, and he doesn't cough it up. At 19.5 years old there is meaningful development ahead, and Phoenix — who acquired him via the thirtieth pick — has a genuine need for young frontcourt talent.

The nine-cat problem is severe. 0.2 made threes. 0.6 steals. 0.7 blocks. 62.3% from the line. That is three dead categories plus an active liability, from a player whose scoring volume isn't high enough to compensate. His composite score of −3.10 is among the lowest on this board, and it is not a low-minutes artifact the way de Larrea's or Lopez's are — he played 27.8 minutes a night.

The boards split sharply on him. Dizzle and Roto both have him twenty-sixth and Angel thirty-first, but Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS disagree with each other: CBS's Adam Finkelstein has him eleventh in this class, Sporting News twenty-third and Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor thirty-fifth. A twenty-four-spot bid-ask spread is one of the widest in the class.

The read. At $30.50, twenty-seventh on the board. The age and the pedigree are legitimate reasons to be interested and the efficiency inside is a real skill. But in a nine-category format, a forward with no threes, no stocks and a broken free throw stroke has to become a genuinely elite scorer to be rosterable, and nothing in the freshman profile suggests that path. SELL is a rating on category fit, not on the player's NBA future.
