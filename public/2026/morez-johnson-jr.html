SCOUTING REPORT

The efficiency is the headline. 13.1 points and 7.3 rebounds on 62.3% shooting in only 25.1 minutes, with 1.1 blocks and just 1.3 turnovers. Per minute, that is a genuinely productive big man, and the 78.2% free throw shooting is unusually good for the archetype — most bigs at this efficiency level are a liability at the line. He also played 40 games, the most of any top-ten prospect, which is a durability signal worth something in a head-to-head format.

He rose meaningfully through the pre-draft process, and the reasons given were consistent: a winning college season, strong rebounding, versatile defense, elite intangibles, and a combine where he measured and tested well. Dallas took him ninth, reuniting him with Dusty May. Physically he is described as NBA-ready now, which is not true of most 20-year-old bigs.

The nine-cat ceiling is the constraint. He contributes to four categories. He is a zero in threes, a zero in assists, and below median in steals. In a format that rewards breadth, a four-category big has to be enormous in those four to justify a roster spot, and 13 and 7 in 25 minutes is good rather than enormous. His class-wide nine-cat composite score of −1.08 reflects exactly that: the shape is narrow even though the quality is real.

The read. Priced at $149.42, eleventh on the board — Angel, Dizzle and Roto cluster around ninth and tenth and Yahoo has him eleventh, while Sporting News has him eighteenth and CBS twenty-first in this class. He is the safest floor of anyone in the 9–15 range and simultaneously has the lowest ceiling. If Dallas gives him 28 minutes he is a useful rebounds-and-percentage piece for a decade. He will not win you a category by himself, and he will not appreciate the way the guards around him might.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 11th, 18th and 21st in this class. Yahoo is the high mark at 11th and CBS the low at 21st.
