SCOUTING REPORT

The production deserves acknowledgment before the situation buries it. 19.8 points, 4.4 assists, 2.6 rebounds, 1.4 steals and 2.5 made threes in 37.7 minutes — the highest minutes load of anyone in this class — with 84.8% free throw shooting and only 1.8 turnovers. That free throw mark is the second best in the top twenty and the turnover discipline at that usage is real. His nine-cat composite of +1.22 ranks twenty-fifth in the class.

Memphis drafted him sixteenth and immediately traded him to Oklahoma City, which is roughly the worst outcome available. The Thunder's guard rotation is the deepest in the league and there is no version of the 2026-27 season in which a rookie second-unit guard finds 20 minutes there. His Pathway to Early Minutes score of 29 is third lowest among first-rounders on this board.

Age compounds it. He is 22.8, which means the years he spends waiting are prime years rather than development years. A 19-year-old blocked for two seasons emerges at 21; Stirtz emerges at 25 with his athletic peak partly spent. That is the difference between a stash and a sunk cost, and it is why the model's Long-Term Starter Upside score docks him hard.

The read. At $68.95 he is priced eighteenth. Dizzle and Roto both have him sixteenth on production; Angel has him twenty-fourth on situation, and Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS have him fifteenth to nineteenth. That nine-spot spread is the argument in miniature. In a 10-team league where roster spots are scarce, a 23-year-old who cannot get on the floor is the most expensive kind of hold there is.

The Yahoo, Sporting News and CBS boards. Yahoo's Kevin O'Connor, Sporting News's Elliott Pohnl and CBS's Adam Finkelstein have him 18th, 15th and 19th in this class.
