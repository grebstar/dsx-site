SCOUTING REPORT

Carter has the most complete statistical line of any guard in this draft: 19.1 points, 8.4 rebounds, 3.5 assists, 1.7 steals and 0.9 blocks with 2.5 made threes, at Providence. In a nine-category format that is six columns filled from one roster slot, and a guard rebounding at 8.4 is genuinely rare. The boards were tight on him — RookieScale 10th, Rotoworld 13th, NBA Draft Room 14th.

The discount is age and health. He turned 22 in March, which caps the development runway, and he had left shoulder surgery in July that ruled him out of both Summer League slates and threatened the start of his season. There is no post-draft sample of any kind, so there is no post-Vegas re-rating here at all.

Sacramento is a middling situation: De'Aaron Fox and Malik Monk are established, and the Kings were pushing for the play-in rather than developing. Pathway 58 against a model base of 80 reflects both the depth chart and a shoulder that will not be right until midseason. The line is worth paying for; the year-one delivery is not.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 19.1 / 8.4 / 3.5 with 1.7 steals — six categories filled from one guard slot
  • 8.4 rebounds from a 6'2" guard is one of the strangest and best marks in the draft
  • 2.5 made threes at 37.7%; the shooting supports the counting stats
  • Defensive versatility rare enough that every board had him top 14

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Left shoulder surgery in July; no Summer League and no post-draft sample
  • 22.3 years old, the oldest guard in the lottery, with a short runway
  • Fox and Monk are entrenched on a team trying to win now
  • 2.6 turnovers and 47.3% from the field on heavy college usage
