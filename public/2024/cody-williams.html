SCOUTING REPORT

Williams shot 55.2% from the field and 41.5% from three at Colorado, which are the numbers of a far better prospect than the rest of the line supports. The boards had him anywhere from 6th to 17th — RookieScale 8th, Rotoworld 10th, NBA Draft Room 17th — and the disagreement is about whether the efficiency is a skill or a consequence of never being asked to do anything difficult.

He is 6'8" with a 7'1" wingspan, he moves well enough to defend three positions, and 0.7 blocks from a wing is a real contribution. Utah is the emptiest depth chart in the league and will play him immediately.

The category objection is volume. 11.9 points on eight shots, 3.0 rebounds, 1.6 assists, 0.6 steals: there is nothing here that wins a week, and 2.0 turnovers against 1.6 assists is a poor ratio for a low-usage player. He also played only 24 games, missing time with an ankle and then a wrist. You are buying frame, age and percentages, and hoping usage follows.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 55.2% from the field and 41.5% from three — elite freshman efficiency
  • 6'8" with a 7'1" wingspan and 0.7 blocks from the wing
  • Utah has no incumbent at the position and every reason to start him
  • Nineteen years old with two years of runway before it has to mean anything

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 11.9 points on eight shots — no category volume anywhere
  • 2.0 turnovers against 1.6 assists from a low-usage role
  • Only 24 college games; the efficiency sample is small and injury-shortened
  • 25.9% from three across seven Summer League games
