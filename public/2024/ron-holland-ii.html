SCOUTING REPORT

Holland is the highest-variance profile in the lottery and the boards knew it: Rotoworld ranked him 5th, RookieScale 11th, NBA Draft Room 11th. What every one of them was staring at is the steal column. 2.5 steals a game for G League Ignite at 18, against paid professionals, is the second-highest rate in this draft behind Reed Sheppard — and Sheppard was doing it in college.

Around it sits a genuinely large statistical season: 20.6 points and 6.6 rebounds in 33.6 minutes, with 3.0 assists. He got to the rim whenever he wanted and drew fouls at a real clip. Detroit is a rebuild with nothing at the position and no reason to sit him.

Then the shot. 24.0% from three on 0.9 makes and 75.7% from the line, with 2.9 turnovers against 3.0 assists. That is not a jumper that is one summer away; that is a player NBA defences will simply refuse to guard beyond the arc, which collapses the driving lane that produces everything else. He is priced 6th here on the strength of the defensive categories, and the offer assumes the volume survives contact with a real rotation. It is the biggest bet on this board that a college hole closes in the NBA.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 2.5 steals per game at 18 against professionals — the scarcest category in the format
  • 20.6 and 6.6 in 33.6 minutes; the raw production is lottery-sized
  • Detroit's rebuild has nothing ahead of him and every reason to play him
  • Rotoworld ranked him 5th, six spots above his RookieScale placement

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 24.0% from three on 0.9 makes — a dead category and a shrinking driving lane
  • 2.9 turnovers against 3.0 assists; the decision-making is not there
  • 44.5% from the field despite living at the rim
  • Only 14 Ignite games — the smallest pre-draft sample of any lottery pick
