SCOUTING REPORT

Holmes is the best statistical big available after the lottery and the boards said so — Rotoworld 21st, RookieScale 27th. The Dayton line is a category manager's shape: 20.4 points, 8.5 rebounds and 2.1 blocks on 54.4% from the field with 38.6% from three on a make a game. Blocks plus threes from one frontcourt slot is the rarest combination in the format.

Phoenix took him 22nd and immediately sent his rights to Denver for Ryan Dunn and two future seconds, which tells you what one front office thought the gap was. Denver is a good landing spot in the medium term: Nikola Jokić is immovable, but the Nuggets have almost nothing behind him and a clear need for a backup big who can shoot.

The costs are age and level. He is 21.9, a three-year college player, and the Atlantic 10 is not the Big Ten — the 2.1 blocks came against smaller opponents than he will now face nightly. Year one is a bench role behind an MVP. Priced 23rd, three spots behind Kyle Filipowski and one ahead of Ryan Dunn, the player he was traded for.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 2.1 blocks and 38.6% from three — the rarest category pair in the draft
  • 20.4 and 8.5 on 54.4% shooting; the production is lottery-grade
  • Denver traded up for him, giving up a first-round pick and two seconds
  • Rotoworld ranked him 21st, one spot above his draft slot

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Nikola Jokić plays his position and does not sit
  • 21.9 years old after three college seasons; limited growth left to buy
  • Atlantic 10 competition inflates both the blocks and the efficiency
  • 2.2 turnovers and 71.3% from the line
