SCOUTING REPORT

Sheppard's Kentucky freshman season is one of the strangest and best statistical profiles in recent memory: 52.1% from three, 53.6% from the field, 83.1% from the line, with 4.5 assists and 2.5 steals. He shot better from behind the arc than most players shoot inside it, and he did it while producing the highest steal rate in the class.

In a nine-category format that is close to the ideal shape — three percentage categories, threes, assists and the scarcest counting stat in the game, all from one roster slot. NBA Draft Room ranked him first in the class and Rotoworld third.

Houston is the problem, and it is a big one. The Rockets won 41 games the year before with a crowded young backcourt and were pushing hard for the playoffs. There is no version of 2024-25 where a 20-year-old rookie guard gets 28 minutes there, and the Pathway score of 54 against a model base of 90 is the largest downgrade anywhere on this board. You are buying the shooting and waiting.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 52.1% from three is the best shooting season by any guard in this draft by a wide margin
  • 2.5 steals per game — the scarcest category in the format, at the highest rate in the class
  • 4.5 assists and 83.1% from the line; there is no dead category anywhere
  • NBA Draft Room ranked him first overall in the class

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Houston is a win-now roster with a crowded backcourt and no minutes to spare
  • Physically slight; the defensive playmaking may not survive NBA size
  • The three-point percentage is unsustainable and will regress hard
  • Largest opportunity downgrade on the board — 36 points of Pathway override
