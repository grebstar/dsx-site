SCOUTING REPORT

Knecht is one of the widest disagreements on the board: RookieScale ranked him 7th and Rotoworld ranked him 23rd, a sixteen-place gap. Both are defensible, which is the interesting part.

The bull number is the scoring. 21.7 points a game with 2.6 made threes at 39.7% in the SEC, plus 4.9 rebounds, is the most polished offensive season in the draft after Zach Edey's. He is a finished product: a movement shooter who can score immediately in any rotation, and Los Angeles has a roster built to feed exactly that player.

The bear number is 23.2 years old. That is a senior with no development curve left, and the rest of the line is thin — 1.8 assists, 0.7 steals, 0.6 blocks. In a dynasty format you are buying the last four years of a player's growth, and Knecht arrives having already spent them. He wins one category, threes, and does nothing else. Priced 11th here, which splits the difference the boards could not.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 21.7 points with 2.6 made threes at 39.7% — the most NBA-ready scoring in the class
  • Immediately useful on a Lakers roster that needs shooting around its stars
  • 4.9 rebounds from the wing keeps a second column from being empty
  • RookieScale ranked him 7th, ten spots above his draft slot

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 23.2 years old with essentially no development runway left
  • 1.8 assists, 0.7 steals, 0.6 blocks — a one-category player in a nine-category format
  • Rotoworld ranked him 23rd; the fantasy market largely passed
  • 36.7% shooting across six Summer League games
