SCOUTING REPORT

Walter arrived at Baylor as a five-star recruit and shot 37.6% from the field, which is the single ugliest efficiency number of any first-round pick in this class. The boards read it very differently: RookieScale 16th, Rotoworld 30th.

The optimistic reading is in the two indicators that usually predict shooting better than field goal percentage does: 2.1 made threes at 34.1% on high volume, and 79.2% from the free-throw line. He also gave 4.4 rebounds and 1.1 steals from the shooting guard slot, which is more than most 19-year-old scorers contribute.

The pessimistic reading is that a freshman taking eleven shots to score fourteen points is a player whose shot selection has to be rebuilt before anything else matters, and rebuilding shot selection is slow. Toronto is rebuilding and will give him the runway. His Las Vegas showing — 21.4% from three across five games — did nothing to help, which is why the post-Vegas mark here is negative.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 79.2% from the line and 2.1 made threes — the shooting indicators are better than the field goal percentage
  • 4.4 rebounds and 1.1 steals from a shooting guard at 19
  • Toronto is rebuilding and can afford to let him shoot through it
  • RookieScale ranked him 16th, three spots above his draft slot

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 37.6% from the field — the worst efficiency of any first-round pick in the class
  • 1.4 assists; he is a scorer who does not yet create for anyone else
  • 21.4% from three across five Summer League games
  • Rotoworld ranked him 30th, eleven spots below where Toronto took him
