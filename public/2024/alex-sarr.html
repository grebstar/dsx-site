SCOUTING REPORT

Rotoworld ranked Sarr first in the entire class, ahead of the player Atlanta took first overall, and the reasoning is the reasoning that governs every category league: blocks are scarce, young mobile centers who block shots are scarcer, and Washington had nothing to keep him off the floor.

The Perth line is small in raw terms — 9.4 points and 4.3 rebounds in 17.3 minutes — but 1.5 blocks in those minutes is a rate that projects to something serious with a starter's workload, and he did it at 18 in a real professional league while shooting 51.6%.

Two things argue against paying up. He shot 28.6% from three and 71.0% from the line, so the floor-spacing case is aspirational. And his Summer League was genuinely alarming — 19.1% from the field across four games, the worst mark of any lottery pick that July, which is why his post-Vegas re-rating is negative despite the board consensus.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Rotoworld's No. 1 player in the class, ahead of the actual first overall pick
  • 1.5 blocks in only 17.3 minutes — the per-minute rim protection is already elite
  • Washington has no incumbent center and every reason to play him 30 minutes
  • 51.6% from the field at 18 against grown professionals in the Australian NBL

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 19.1% from the field across four Summer League games
  • 28.6% from three and 71.0% from the line — the shooting is not there yet
  • 0.9 assists and 0.4 steals; outside blocks the profile is narrow
  • Washington will be historically bad, which caps team-context value
