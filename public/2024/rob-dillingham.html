SCOUTING REPORT

Dillingham's Kentucky freshman year is the most efficient scoring season by any guard in this class: 15.2 points in 23.2 minutes on 47.5% from the field and 44.4% from three, with 2.0 made threes and 3.9 assists. He was a sixth man who outproduced most starters, and every board had him inside the top 15 — Rotoworld 6th, NBA Draft Room 6th, RookieScale 12th.

The scoring translates in shape as well as size: he creates his own looks, he shoots off movement, and the 79.6% free-throw mark supports the jumper. In Las Vegas he added 7.6 assists a game, which is the highest mark of any rookie that July and suggests the playmaking is better than the college usage let on.

The problem is arithmetic. San Antonio drafted him and sent his rights to Minnesota, a 56-win roster with Mike Conley, Donte DiVincenzo and Nickeil Alexander-Walker already in front of him. There is no minutes path in year one and probably not in year two. He is also 6'2" and 175 pounds with 0.1 blocks and real defensive questions, which is exactly the kind of guard a good team hides on the bench. Pathway 52 against a model base of 86 is the second-largest downgrade on this board.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 44.4% from three on 2.0 makes — the best shooting season by any guard in the class
  • 15.2 points in 23.2 minutes; the per-minute scoring is already NBA-grade
  • 7.6 assists a game in Summer League, the highest of any 2024 rookie
  • Every board ranked him inside the top 15, two of them 6th

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Minnesota won 56 games and has three guards ahead of him
  • 6'2", 175 pounds with 0.1 blocks — a defensive target on a contender
  • 3.4 turnovers a game in Las Vegas against 7.6 assists
  • The second-largest opportunity downgrade on this board
