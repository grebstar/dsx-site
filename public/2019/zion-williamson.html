SCOUTING REPORT

New Orleans took him first overall out of Duke. He had just gone for 22.6 points, 8.9 rebounds and 2.1 assists in 33 games at 30.0 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #1, pick 1. All 5 boards had him at first. There was no disagreement to price.

2019-20: 24 games, 24 starts, 27.8 minutes — 22.5 / 6.3 / 2.1 on 58.3% shooting. That ranked him third in the class and marked him at $623.16, -37.7% against his IPO. 2025-26: 62 games, 55 starts, 29.7 minutes — 21.0 / 5.7 / 3.2 on 60.0% shooting. That ranked him second in the class and marked him at $808.24, -19.2% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $1,000.00, today's mark of $808.24 is -19.2%. The board reads that as MARKET GOT IT RIGHT. Holding the top rung against a whole class trying to take it is the hardest outcome available here, not a neutral one. He finished third in the All-Rookie vote among 2019 draftees.

He has never been traded. New Orleans made the pick on Jun. 20, 2019 and every season row on his record reads New Orleans. He signed a five-year rookie-scale maximum extension on Jul. 6, 2022, running through 2027-28, and it is written with games-played and conditioning triggers rather than as a flat guarantee. The reported value is not stated here: one account puts the base at $193 million, another at $197.2 million, and both describe escalators reaching about $231 million. The page will not pick between them.

2025-26 was the healthiest season of his career by appearances. He played 62 games and put up 21.0 / 5.7 / 3.2, and a sprained ankle against Utah on Mar. 1, 2026 ended a 35-game run of consecutive appearances. New Orleans finished 26-56, eleventh in the West, and missed the playoffs. At his end-of-season press conference in April, Joe Dumars said the Pelicans had no intention of trading him.
