SCOUTING REPORT

Boston took him #20 overall out of Washington. He had just gone for 9.1 points, 3.1 rebounds and 2.1 assists in 36 games at 31.1 minutes a night. He went 9 picks ahead of his composite, which was #29. The boards ranged from #22 to #60 on him, a spread of 38 places across 5 of them.

2019-20: 65 games, 14 starts, 19.8 minutes — 4.7 / 1.6 / 1.2 on 42.3% shooting. That ranked him tenth in the class and marked him at $170.70, +567.5% against his IPO. 2025-26: 30 games, 0 starts, 16.0 minutes — 5.8 / 2.0 / 0.9 on 43.3% shooting. That ranked him #23 in the class and marked him at $43.38, +69.6% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $25.57, today's mark of $43.38 is +69.6%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished tenth in the All-Rookie vote among 2019 draftees.

Boston selected him twentieth on Jun. 20, 2019 and traded his rights to Philadelphia the same night for the No. 24 pick that became Ty Jerome and the No. 33 pick that became Carsen Edwards. Boston then sent Jerome and Aron Baynes on to Phoenix for a 2020 first.

In February 2023 he reached Portland in the same run of paperwork that sent Cam Reddish there and Jalen McDaniels to Philadelphia, with Josh Hart going to New York. Basketball-Reference and NBA.com record one four-team trade; CBS, ESPN and KGW record a two-team Hart-for-Reddish deal and a separate three-team deal a day apart. The three pages this touches name all three men and none asserts a transaction count.

2025-26 was 30 games and no starts at 5.8 / 2.0 with 2.0 steals in 16.0 minutes, on .398 from three. He tore the UCL in his left thumb against Utah in late October and had surgery on Oct. 30, 2025 with a four-to-six week reevaluation. Portland finished 42-40, eighth in the West, and lost the first round to San Antonio 4-1; he played four playoff games at 1.8 a game. He signed a one-year deal with the Lakers on Jul. 20, 2026; the figure is carried by one outlet and is not stated.
