SCOUTING REPORT

Cleveland took him fifth overall out of Vanderbilt. He had just gone for 16.2 points, 3.8 rebounds and 2.6 assists in 5 games at 27.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #8, pick 5. The boards ranged from fifth to #13 on him, a spread of 8 places across 5 of them.

2019-20: 59 games, 59 starts, 30.9 minutes — 12.3 / 1.9 / 3.9 on 40.1% shooting. That ranked him eleventh in the class and marked him at $149.42, -34.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 45 games, 43 starts, 29.9 minutes — 18.8 / 2.4 / 6.7 on 46.0% shooting. That ranked him fourth in the class and marked him at $495.29, +116.2% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $229.10, today's mark of $495.29 is +116.2%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished eleventh in the All-Rookie vote among 2019 draftees.

Cleveland took him fifth on Jun. 20, 2019 and kept him six and a half seasons. In early February 2026 the Cavaliers sent him and a future second-round pick to the LA Clippers for James Harden; CBS dates the deal Feb. 3 and NBA.com Feb. 4-5, so no date is stated here. He has two years left on the five-year rookie-scale maximum extension he signed in 2022, and the trade cost him supermax eligibility. The remaining salary is single-sourced and is not stated.

He was already out when the trade happened. A toe problem carried over from the 2025 postseason had sidelined him since Jan. 14, and he did not debut for the Clippers until Mar. 2, 2026 against Golden State, after which he ran the offense at a 29.8% usage rate. Los Angeles finished 42-40 and ninth in the West and lost a win-or-go-home play-in game to Golden State.
