SCOUTING REPORT

Oklahoma City took him #21 overall out of Gonzaga. He had just gone for 16.9 points, 8.6 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 37 games at 28.1 minutes a night. The market liked him a great deal more than the room did: composite #9, drafted 21. The boards ranged from eighth to #23 on him, a spread of 15 places across 5 of them.

2019-20: 58 games, 4 starts, 22.4 minutes — 12.1 / 5.9 / 1.4 on 61.8% shooting. That ranked him second in the class and marked him at $808.24, +310.7% against his IPO. 2025-26: 2 games, 2 starts, 10.0 minutes — 4.0 / 3.0 / 0.5 on 33.3% shooting. That ranked him #18 in the class and marked him at $68.95, -65.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $196.79, today's mark of $68.95 is -65.0%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished second in the All-Rookie vote among 2019 draftees.

Oklahoma City selected him twenty-first on Jun. 20, 2019 and traded his rights to Memphis for the No. 23 pick that became Darius Bazley and a 2024 second. He was never traded again and played all seven of his NBA seasons in Memphis, on a four-year, $52 million extension agreed in October 2022 that ran through 2026-27.

He had knee surgery in the 2025 offseason and did not debut until Dec. 17, 2025 at Minnesota — 17 minutes, six points, three rebounds. He played once more, against Washington on Dec. 20, and left with a Grade 2 right calf strain. Memphis ruled him out for the remainder of the season on Mar. 25, 2026 and finished 25-57. Brandon Clarke died on May 11, 2026. He was 29. The NBA, the Grizzlies and the players' association each issued statements.

The mark on his 2025-26 card is computed from his 2024-25 season rather than from those two games, and the card says so. Two games in December are not a verdict the market passed on him.
