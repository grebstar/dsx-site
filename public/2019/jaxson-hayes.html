SCOUTING REPORT

Atlanta took him eighth overall out of Texas. He had just gone for 10.0 points, 5.0 rebounds and 0.3 assists in 32 games at 23.3 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #6, pick 8. The boards ranged from fourth to eleventh on him, a spread of 7 places across 5 of them.

2019-20: 64 games, 14 starts, 16.9 minutes — 7.4 / 4.0 / 0.9 on 67.2% shooting. That ranked him #13 in the class and marked him at $116.93, -63.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 66 games, 9 starts, 18.3 minutes — 7.5 / 4.1 / 0.9 on 75.6% shooting. That ranked him #21 in the class and marked him at $51.92, -83.9% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $322.90, today's mark of $51.92 is -83.9%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished #13 in the All-Rookie vote among 2019 draftees.

He was drafted by a team he never played for. Atlanta selected him eighth on Jun. 20, 2019 and sent him, the No. 17 pick that became Nickeil Alexander-Walker and the No. 35 pick that became Didi Louzada, plus a protected 2020 first, to New Orleans; back came the No. 4 pick that became De'Andre Hunter, Solomon Hill and the No. 57 pick that became Jordan Bone. Five members of this class in one deal. He has not been traded since.

He left New Orleans as a free agent for the Lakers in 2023 and left the Lakers as a free agent in July 2026, signing with Utah for two years and $12 million with a team option on the second. In 2025-26 he played 66 games at 7.5 / 4.1 / 0.9 in 18.3 minutes, backing up Deandre Ayton and starting when Ayton was out. Los Angeles finished 53-29, beat Houston 4-2 and was swept by Oklahoma City in the conference semifinals.
