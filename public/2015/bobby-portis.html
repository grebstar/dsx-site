SCOUTING REPORT

Chicago took him #22 overall out of Arkansas. He had just gone for 17.5 points, 8.9 rebounds and 1.2 assists in 36 games at 29.9 minutes a night. The boards had him 6 places above where he went, at #16. The boards ranged from twelfth to #21 on him, a spread of 9 places across 12 of them.

2015-16: 62 games, 4 starts, 17.8 minutes — 7.0 / 5.4 / 0.8 on 42.7% shooting. That ranked him #14 in the class and marked him at $104.27, +23.9% against his IPO. 2025-26: 67 games, 9 starts, 24.2 minutes — 13.7 / 6.4 / 1.6 on 48.8% shooting. That ranked him sixth in the class and marked him at $322.90, +283.7% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $84.15, today's mark of $322.90 is +283.7%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished #14 in the All-Rookie vote among 2015 draftees.

Chicago took him 22nd and kept him three and a half seasons. On Feb. 6, 2019 he went to Washington with Jabari Parker for Otto Porter. Washington withdrew his qualifying offer in July 2019 and he signed a two-year deal with the Knicks, who declined his option after one season. He signed with Milwaukee in November 2020 and was part of the 2021 championship team, then re-signed with the Bucks in 2021, on a four-year, $49 million deal in 2022, and on a three-year, $44 million deal in 2025 that carries a 2027-28 player option.

The NBA suspended him 25 games on Feb. 20, 2025 for a banned substance; his agent said he took Tramadol believing it was Toradol. On Jul. 6, 2026 Milwaukee sent him and Giannis Antetokounmpo to Miami for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, the No. 13 pick in the 2026 draft (Nate Ament) and future picks.
