Minnesota took him first overall out of Kentucky. He had just gone for 10.3 points, 6.7 rebounds and 1.1 assists in 39 games at 21.1 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #1, pick 1. The boards ranged from first to second on him, a spread of 1 places across 12 of them.

Against a frozen IPO of $1,000.00, today's mark of $808.24 is -19.2%. The board reads that as MARKET GOT IT RIGHT — he is priced about where the consensus put him. He finished first in the All-Rookie vote among 2015 draftees.

Minnesota took him first and kept him for nine seasons. He signed a rookie-scale extension on Sep. 23, 2018 and a veteran extension on Jul. 7, 2022, made four All-Star teams as a Timberwolf (2018, 2019, 2022, 2024) and won the 2022 Three-Point Contest.

On Oct. 2, 2024 Minnesota traded him to New York, with the draft rights to James Nnaji, for Julius Randle, Donte DiVincenzo, Keita Bates-Diop, Charlie Brown Jr., DaQuan Jeffries, Duane Washington Jr., draft compensation and cash. He made All-NBA Third Team in 2025, his third selection, and in 2025-26 won the NBA Cup in December and the championship in June, when the Knicks beat San Antonio 4-1 in the Finals.

He holds a $61 million player option for 2027-28. In September 2026 ESPN reported that he and the Knicks were “tens of millions apart” on an extension worth up to $276 million over four years.
