SCOUTING REPORT

the Lakers took him second overall out of Ohio State. He had just gone for 19.3 points, 5.7 rebounds and 5.0 assists in 35 games at 33.9 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #3, pick 2. The boards ranged from second to fourth on him, a spread of 2 places across 12 of them.

2015-16: 80 games, 48 starts, 28.2 minutes — 13.2 / 3.4 / 3.3 on 41.0% shooting. That ranked him sixth in the class and marked him at $322.90, -48.2% against his IPO. 2025-26: 26 games, 3 starts, 19.0 minutes — 10.2 / 2.3 / 4.0 on 40.5% shooting. That ranked him eighth in the class and marked him at $229.10, -63.2% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $623.16, today's mark of $229.10 is -63.2%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished sixth in the All-Rookie vote among 2015 draftees.

The Lakers took him second and traded him on Jun. 22, 2017 to Brooklyn, with Timofey Mozgov, for Brook Lopez and the draft rights to Kyle Kuzma. He made the 2019 All-Star team as a Net, then went to Golden State on Jul. 7, 2019 in the sign-and-trade that sent Kevin Durant to Brooklyn. Golden State moved him to Minnesota on Feb. 6, 2020 for Andrew Wiggins and picks, and Minnesota sent him back to the Lakers on Feb. 9, 2023, with Malik Beasley and Jarred Vanderbilt, in the deadline deal that moved Russell Westbrook out of Los Angeles.

The Lakers traded him to Brooklyn again on Dec. 29, 2024 for Dorian Finney-Smith and Shake Milton. He agreed to a two-year, $13 million deal with Dallas in June 2025. On Feb. 5, 2026 Dallas sent him, Anthony Davis, Dante Exum and Jaden Hardy to Washington in a multi-team deadline deal that also moved 2015 classmate Tyus Jones from Charlotte to Dallas. He never played for the Wizards, exercised his roughly $6 million option for 2026-27 in June, was traded to Memphis on Jul. 8, 2026, and was waived on Sep. 25, 2026.
