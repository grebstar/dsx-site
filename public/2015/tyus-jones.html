SCOUTING REPORT

Cleveland took him #24 overall out of Duke. He had just gone for 11.8 points, 3.5 rebounds and 5.6 assists in 39 games at 33.9 minutes a night. The boards had him 5 places above where he went, at #19. The boards ranged from #18 to #27 on him, a spread of 9 places across 12 of them.

2015-16: 37 games, 0 starts, 15.5 minutes — 4.2 / 1.3 / 2.9 on 35.9% shooting. That ranked him #24 in the class and marked him at $39.70, -36.6% against his IPO. 2025-26: 67 games, 12 starts, 14.6 minutes — 3.0 / 1.1 / 2.4 on 34.9% shooting. That ranked him tenth in the class and marked him at $170.70, +172.7% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $62.59, today's mark of $170.70 is +172.7%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay.

Cleveland drafted him 24th and sent his rights to Minnesota on draft night for the rights to Cedi Osman (31st) and Rakeem Christmas (36th), both from the same 2015 class, plus a 2019 second. He left as a restricted free agent in July 2019 on a three-year, $24 million offer sheet from Memphis that Minnesota declined to match, and re-signed there for two years and $30 million in 2022. On Jun. 23, 2023 a three-team trade sent him to Washington, with Danilo Gallinari and Mike Muscala arriving from Boston, while Kristaps Porzingis, the No. 4 pick in his class, went from Washington to Boston. He started all 66 games he played that season.

He signed one-year deals with Phoenix (2024) and Orlando (2025). Orlando traded him to Charlotte on Feb. 4, 2026, and a day later he went to Dallas in a three-team deal among Dallas, Washington and Charlotte that sent D'Angelo Russell, the No. 2 pick in his class, from Dallas to Washington with Anthony Davis. Dallas waived him Feb. 28; he signed with Denver on Mar. 5 and re-signed there for one year in July 2026.
