SCOUTING REPORT

the Lakers took him #27 overall out of Wyoming. He had just gone for 16.1 points, 7.2 rebounds and 2.5 assists in 31 games at 34.9 minutes a night. He went 20 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #47, drafted 27. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #40 to #60 on him, a spread of 20 places across 12 of them.

2015-16: 63 games, 22 starts, 20.1 minutes — 5.5 / 5.0 / 0.7 on 52.7% shooting. That ranked him #16 in the class and marked him at $84.15, +3770.4% against his IPO. 2025-26: 35 games, 3 starts, 12.8 minutes — 3.7 / 2.7 / 1.0 on 41.9% shooting. That ranked him twelfth in the class and marked him at $131.64, +5955.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $2.17, today's mark of $131.64 is +5955.0%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished #16 in the All-Rookie vote among 2015 draftees.

The Lakers took him 27th and signed him on Jul. 10, 2015. On Feb. 8, 2018 they traded him to Cleveland with Jordan Clarkson for Isaiah Thomas, Channing Frye and a 2018 first-round pick; he signed a rookie-scale extension with the Cavaliers that October. On Aug. 28, 2021 he went to Portland in a three-team deal that sent Lauri Markkanen to Cleveland and Derrick Jones Jr. and a protected Portland first to Chicago.

Portland moved him to New Orleans on Feb. 8, 2022 with C.J. McCollum and Tony Snell, for Josh Hart, Nickeil Alexander-Walker, Tomas Satoransky, Didi Louzada and picks. He extended with the Pelicans in October 2022, then was traded to Atlanta on Jul. 6, 2024 with Dyson Daniels, E.J. Liddell, Cody Zeller and two first-round picks for Dejounte Murray. He returned to Cleveland as a free agent in July 2025 and signed a one-year, $4 million deal with Indiana in July 2026. Through 2025-26 he had played 581 regular-season games.
