SCOUTING REPORT

Cleveland took him first overall out of Kansas. He had just gone for 17.1 points, 5.9 rebounds and 1.5 assists in 35 games at 32.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #1, pick 1. The boards ranged from first to third on him, a spread of 2 places across 12 of them.

2014-15: 82 games, 82 starts, 36.2 minutes — 16.9 / 4.6 / 2.1 on 43.7% shooting. That ranked him first in the class and marked him at $1,000.00, +0.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: 68 games, 68 starts, 30.3 minutes — 15.4 / 4.8 / 2.7 on 47.5% shooting. That ranked him fourth in the class and marked him at $495.29, -50.5% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $1,000.00, today's mark of $495.29 is -50.5%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished first in the All-Rookie vote among 2014 draftees.

Cleveland took him first and never played him. He signed on Jul. 24, 2014, and on Aug. 23, 2014 went to Minnesota with Anthony Bennett in the three-team trade, with Philadelphia, that sent Kevin Love to Cleveland and also brought Thaddeus Young to Minnesota. He signed a rookie-scale extension on Oct. 11, 2017.

On Feb. 6, 2020 Minnesota traded him to Golden State, with a top-3-protected 2021 first and a 2021 second, for D'Angelo Russell, Jacob Evans and Omari Spellman. He was a 2022 All-Star and won the 2022 championship with the Warriors, and signed a veteran extension on Oct. 15, 2022.

On Feb. 6, 2025 he went to Miami with fellow 2014 draftee Kyle Anderson and a 2025 first-round pick in the five-team trade that sent Jimmy Butler to Golden State; Davion Mitchell also came to Miami from Toronto. In January 2026 the Warriors explored bringing him back for Jonathan Kuminga, but he stayed. On Jun. 29, 2026 he agreed to a three-year, $64 million deal: he exercised his $30.2 million option for 2026-27 and added $16.5 million for 2027-28 and a $17.5 million player option for 2028-29.
