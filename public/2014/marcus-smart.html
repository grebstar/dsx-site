SCOUTING REPORT

Boston took him sixth overall out of Oklahoma State. He had just gone for 18.0 points, 5.9 rebounds and 4.8 assists in 31 games at 32.7 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #6, pick 6. The boards ranged from fifth to eleventh on him, a spread of 6 places across 12 of them.

2014-15: 67 games, 38 starts, 27.0 minutes — 7.8 / 3.3 / 3.1 on 36.7% shooting. That ranked him fourth in the class and marked him at $495.29, +53.4% against his IPO. 2025-26: 62 games, 54 starts, 28.5 minutes — 9.3 / 2.8 / 3.0 on 39.5% shooting. That ranked him ninth in the class and marked him at $196.79, -39.1% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $322.90, today's mark of $196.79 is -39.1%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished fourth in the All-Rookie vote among 2014 draftees.

Boston took him sixth and kept him for nine seasons. He re-signed as a restricted free agent on Jul. 19, 2018, signed a veteran extension on Aug. 21, 2021, and was named Defensive Player of the Year for 2021-22, one of three All-Defensive First Team selections (2019, 2020, 2022).

On Jun. 23, 2023 Boston sent him to Memphis in a three-team trade with Washington that brought Kristaps Porzingis to Boston and sent Tyus Jones from Memphis to Washington. He played 20 games for Memphis in 2023-24. On Feb. 6, 2025 Memphis traded him, with a 2025 first-round pick, to Washington for Marvin Bagley III, Johnny Davis and two seconds, in a deal that also involved Sacramento.

Washington waived him on Jul. 20, 2025 and he signed with the Lakers on Jul. 22. He declined his $5.4 million player option for 2026-27 and agreed on Jul. 1, 2026 to a two-year, $13 million contract with Houston, signed on Jul. 10.
