SCOUTING REPORT

Newell's Georgia freshman season is quietly efficient across the board: 15.4 points and 6.9 rebounds on 54.3% shooting, with 1.0 steals and 1.0 blocks and only 1.1 turnovers. A big who fills both defensive categories while shooting better than 54% and protecting the ball is a clean nine-cat holding, and FantasyPros ranked him 14th on that basis.

RotoWire had him 28th. The gap is about what he does not do: 0.9 assists is essentially a dead category, and 29.2% from three on 0.8 makes means the "stretch" in stretch-four is aspirational rather than established. He is efficient because he only takes shots he is comfortable with, which is a virtue in college and a ceiling in the NBA.

Atlanta is a reasonable landing spot without being a great one. Jalen Johnson holds his natural position, and the Hawks are trying to win now rather than develop, which usually means a rookie big waits.

He is priced in the low twenties because the floor is real and the ceiling depends on a jump shot that has not arrived yet.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 54.3% from the field with 1.0 steals and 1.0 blocks — efficient and two-way at once
  • Only 1.1 turnovers; among the cleanest possession profiles of any big in the class
  • FantasyPros ranked him 14th, nine spots above his draft slot
  • 42.9% from three across four Summer League starts hints the shot may be closer than the college line suggests

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 0.9 assists is a fully dead category for a modern frontcourt player
  • 29.2% from three on 0.8 makes — the floor spacing does not exist yet
  • Jalen Johnson occupies his natural position in Atlanta
  • Efficiency comes partly from shot selection, which usually compresses at the next level
