SCOUTING REPORT

Richardson's efficiency profile is the best of any guard in this draft: 49.3% from the field, 41.2% from three and 83.6% from the line, with only 0.8 turnovers in 25.3 minutes. He came off the bench for most of Michigan State's season and still produced 12.1 points. Shot-making that clean at 19 is not common.

The board consensus was fairly tight — nine of twelve boards had him 17th to 25th — which usually means the market has him correctly placed and there is no edge either way.

What limits him is size and role. At 6'3" he is a shooting guard without shooting-guard length, and 1.9 assists means he is not a point guard either. In a nine-cat format that leaves points, threes and percentages — three categories, all of them the easiest to replace on a waiver wire.

Orlando is a competent, crowded team that will not need him. He is priced in the high teens as an efficient specialist with a narrow path, which is exactly what he is.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 49.3 / 41.2 / 83.6 — the cleanest efficiency line of any guard in the class
  • Only 0.8 turnovers; he does not hurt you anywhere
  • Genuine shot-making pedigree at 19, mostly off the bench
  • 16.5 points on 55.6% across two Summer League games

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 6'3" without shooting-guard length and 1.9 assists — no clear position
  • Points, threes and percentages only; the three most replaceable categories
  • Orlando is competent and crowded with no obvious opening
  • Tight board consensus (nine of twelve boards at 17th-25th) means no market inefficiency to exploit
