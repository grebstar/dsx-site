SCOUTING REPORT

There is no statistical argument for González and everyone involved knows it. He played 14.2 minutes a game in the Spanish ACB and 7.6 in EuroLeague, producing 5.2 points and 2.5 rebounds on 44.4% shooting with 27.1% from three. Across all competitions he averaged 3.5 points. In a format that scores box-score production, that is a blank page.

What you are buying is Real Madrid's development pipeline and a defensive projection. He is 19, physically NBA-ready, and the reason he did not play more is that Real Madrid's rotation is full of grown professionals — a very different reason than not being good enough.

The boards treated him accordingly and consistently: 22nd to 38th across eleven boards, with FantasyPros leaving him off entirely. Nobody ranked him above 22 and nobody dismissed him.

Boston is a 60-win roster with no minutes to spare, so year one is likely another blank page. The Long-Term score of 68 against a model base of 58 is the analyst override: the tools and the age justify a stash even though the production does not justify anything.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 19 years old and already physically NBA-ready; the defensive projection is genuine
  • Real Madrid's rotation, not his ability, is why the minutes were small
  • Boston's development infrastructure and no pressure to play him early
  • 10.8 points and 4.5 rebounds in four Summer League games — more production than his whole ACB season implied

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 5.2 points in 14.2 minutes — there is essentially no statistical case to underwrite
  • 27.1% from three; the shot is not close
  • FantasyPros left him off their board; no source ranked him above 22nd
  • A 60-win Boston roster has no rotation minutes available
