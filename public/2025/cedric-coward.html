SCOUTING REPORT

The framing to avoid is "nobody saw him coming." By June 2025 Coward was 15th on the final consensus scouting board and 10th to 12th on every dynasty list — a well-publicized combine riser with a 7'2" wingspan and a 38.5" vertical, taken 11th. The market knew exactly who he was.

The problem is what the market had to go on. Six games. Coward's Washington State season ended in late November with a left shoulder injury requiring surgery, and the line he produced in that sample — 17.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, 3.7 assists, 1.7 blocks and 2.0 made threes on 55.7% from the field and 83.9% from the line — is spectacular and almost meaningless as a projection. Six games is noise wearing the costume of a scouting report.

If it is real, the profile is a genuine nine-cat contributor: a wing who blocks shots, shoots threes at volume, protects your percentages and passes. That combination is worth far more than pick 11. Memphis needs exactly this player as Desmond Bane's replacement, and the minutes are there.

He is also 21.8 — more than three years older than Flagg and Essengue, which cuts both ways: more NBA-ready now, less runway later. And he will not play Summer League, still rehabbing the shoulder, so there is no bridging sample between the six games and October.

▲ BULL CASE

  • A wing supplying 1.7 blocks AND 2.0 made threes — that combination is genuinely rare
  • 55.7% from the field and 83.9% from the line; the efficiency profile is clean everywhere
  • Memphis has an open wing role after Desmond Bane; the minutes are not in question
  • 7'2" wingspan and 38.5" vertical at the combine — the physical tools are elite

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Six college games. The entire statistical case rests on a sample that would be discarded anywhere else
  • Left shoulder surgery ended his season; he will not play Summer League
  • 21.8 years old — more than three years older than the youngest lottery picks
  • All twelve boards listed him, and the highest ranked him 7th — nobody was aggressive
