SCOUTING REPORT

Bailey slid to five on draft night amid reporting about refused workouts, and the fantasy market treated that slide as a discount rather than a warning. RookieScale, Rotoworld, RotoWire, DraftRoom and FantasyPros had him between second and fifth; RotoWire put him second outright.

The reason is opportunity. Utah is the emptiest depth chart in the league and Bailey is the only player they added who is going to shoot 15 times a night from day one. In a category format, guaranteed usage on a bad team is worth more than efficient usage on a good one, and Bailey has the most of it in this class.

The Rutgers line supports it: 17.6 points and 7.2 rebounds with 1.3 blocks from a wing, which is an unusual and valuable combination — forwards who block shots are how you win that category without punting a roster spot on a center. The shot-making at 6'10" is genuinely rare.

What you are buying against: 1.3 assists is close to a dead category, 69.2% from the line is a real drag in nine-cat, and the shot selection that scouts flagged is visible in the 46.0% field goal percentage. This is a high-variance holding with a wide outcome band, priced fifth because the runway is enormous and the floor of "18 shots a night on a rebuilding team" is worth paying for.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Largest guaranteed usage share of any rookie in the class — Utah has nobody ahead of him
  • 1.3 blocks and 7.2 rebounds from a wing slot is a scarce category combination
  • Ranked 2nd-5th on RookieScale, Rotoworld, RotoWire, DraftRoom and FantasyPros despite going 5th — positive gap to draft capital
  • Shot-making at 6'10" with real three-point volume (1.6 makes/gm as a freshman)

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 1.3 assists is effectively a punted category
  • 69.2% from the line is a meaningful nine-cat drag and does not usually fix itself fast
  • Shot selection concerns were the reason he fell to five — 46.0% FG is the evidence
  • Two Salt Lake City games at 37% before injury; never appeared in Las Vegas
