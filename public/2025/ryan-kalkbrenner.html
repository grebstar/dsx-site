SCOUTING REPORT

Kalkbrenner won four straight Big East Defensive Player of the Year awards — a record fourth, matching Patrick Ewing as the only players ever to do it — plus the 2025 Naismith Defensive Player of the Year and the Kareem Abdul-Jabbar Award as the nation's best center. He holds Big East career records for blocked shots (248) and field goal percentage (.639).

The senior line is enormous: 19.2 points, 8.7 rebounds and 2.7 blocks on 65.3% shooting. In a category format, a center producing that many blocks at that efficiency is a weekly advantage in two columns, and Rotoworld ranked him 15th in the class because of it — ahead of eleven players who went in the first round.

He fell to 34 for two reasons that are both real. He was 23.4 on draft night, the oldest player in this entire build, so there is no development runway to price. And he cannot shoot — 0.6 made threes on 34.4% at tiny volume, in a league moving away from non-shooting bigs.

Charlotte offers something no first-round center in this class got: an actual opening. Mason Plumlee and Moussa Diabate are the competition, and Kalkbrenner is better than both right now. His Pathway score of 80 against a model base of 44 is the biggest positive override on this board.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Four-time Big East Defensive Player of the Year and 2025 Naismith Defensive Player of the Year
  • 2.7 blocks on 65.3% shooting — two elite category contributions from one roster slot
  • Rotoworld ranked him 15th, ahead of eleven first-round picks
  • Charlotte's center rotation is Mason Plumlee and Moussa Diabate; the job is genuinely available

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 23.4 years old — the oldest player on this board, with no development runway
  • 0.6 made threes on tiny volume; a drop-coverage, non-shooting center in a league moving away from both
  • 1.5 assists and 0.5 steals; outside blocks and field goal percentage the profile is empty
  • Second-round contract means no organizational obligation to play him
