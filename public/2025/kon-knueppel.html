SCOUTING REPORT

Knueppel is the one player in the top five where the NBA market and the fantasy market disagreed. Charlotte took him fourth; NBA.com had him seventh among rookies and FantasyPros eighth. The stated objection: efficient but low-usage, with almost nothing outside the shooting.

The shooting itself is not in question. 2.2 made threes per game on 40.6% from deep, with 91.4% from the free-throw line — that free-throw number is the best in the class by a distance and it is the most reliable predictor of shooting translation there is. He was 14.4 / 4.0 / 2.7 with only 1.4 turnovers at Duke, playing next to Flagg.

The nine-cat objection is legitimate. 0.2 blocks and 1.0 steals means he contributes essentially nothing in the two defensive categories, so you are buying points, threes and percentages and punting a third of the format. That is a real structural cost, and it is why he sits fourth on this composite rather than higher.

What the discount ignores is the landing spot. Charlotte has open shot volume, LaMelo Ball to create for him, and no incumbent at his position. This is the lowest-variance offering in the lottery — you know precisely what you are getting — and it comes at no premium to draft slot.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 91.4% from the free-throw line — best in the class and the single best predictor of shooting translation
  • 40.6% on 2.2 made threes per game as a freshman, next to a higher-usage teammate
  • Charlotte offers open shot volume and no incumbent ahead of him
  • Only 1.4 turnovers — lowest-variance profile of any top-five pick

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 0.2 blocks and 1.0 steals — contributes nothing across a third of the format
  • Rotoworld, RotoWire, DraftRoom and FantasyPros ranked him below his draft slot; the low-usage concern was widely shared
  • Athletic ceiling is the lowest of the top five; the upside case is 'very good starter', not star
  • Charlotte's offense runs through LaMelo Ball, which caps the creation share
