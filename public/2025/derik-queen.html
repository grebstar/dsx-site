SCOUTING REPORT

Queen went thirteenth and the fantasy market thought that was absurd. RotoWire ranked him third in the entire class. FantasyPros had him sixth, NBC seventh. Against a #13 draft slot, that is one of the biggest positive gaps on the board — and it exists because the points-rebounds-assists big is worth substantially more in a category format than in an NBA front office.

The Maryland line is the argument: 16.5 points and 9.0 rebounds on 52.6% shooting with 1.1 steals and 1.1 blocks. A big who supplies both defensive categories while shooting better than 52% is filling five columns at once, and the 76.6% from the line means he does not wreck your percentages the way most centers do.

What pushed him down NBA boards — conditioning, defensive mobility, whether he holds up as a full-time five — are real concerns that fantasy managers can largely ignore, because minutes played is the only thing that matters and New Orleans has nobody entrenched ahead of him.

The costs are honest: 0.2 made threes is a dead category and will stay dead for a while, and the Pelicans paid an unprotected future first to move up for him, which raises the organizational pressure to play him immediately. The July wrist surgery is the near-term risk that the offer price already reflects.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Ranked 3rd by RotoWire against a 13th draft slot — among the largest positive gaps in the class
  • 1.1 steals AND 1.1 blocks from a center, on 52.6% shooting — five columns filled at once
  • 76.6% from the line is unusually good for a big and protects your percentages
  • New Orleans traded an unprotected first to get him; the organization is committed to playing him

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 0.2 made threes — a fully dead category with no near-term fix
  • Tore the scapholunate ligament in his left wrist at Summer League; surgery on July 18
  • Conditioning and defensive mobility are the reasons he fell to 13 and they do not vanish
  • Frontcourt fit in New Orleans is crowded, and he is not a floor-spacer
