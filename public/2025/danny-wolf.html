SCOUTING REPORT

Wolf is the most unusual offensive player in this draft: a legitimate seven-footer who ran ball screens as the handler at Michigan and put up 13.2 points, 9.7 rebounds and 3.6 assists with 1.4 blocks and 1.0 made threes. Five categories at once from the center slot is rare enough that seven of twelve boards ranked him 14th to 20th, comfortably above his #27 draft slot.

The cost is written right next to it. 3.2 turnovers is the worst figure in the entire draft, worse than any guard, and it is structural — a big man handling in traffic against NBA length will cough it up more, not less. Pair that with 59.4% from the free-throw line and you have two categories actively working against you every week.

He is also a positional tweener that three different sources list three different ways: Basketball-Reference says power forward, NBA.com says forward, RealGM says center. That ambiguity is a feature in a category league — it is roster flexibility — and a liability in an NBA rotation.

Brooklyn is the ideal home for it. The Nets have no timeline pressure and drafted five players in this class; Wolf will get the ball and be allowed to make mistakes with it.

▲ BULL CASE

  • A seven-footer producing 3.6 assists — genuinely scarce, and it fills a category bigs never touch
  • 9.7 rebounds with 1.4 blocks and 1.0 made threes; five columns filled from one roster slot
  • Seven of twelve boards ranked him 14th-20th against a #27 draft slot
  • Brooklyn has no timeline pressure and will let him play through mistakes

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 3.2 turnovers is the worst mark in the draft and structural to how he plays
  • 59.4% from the line is a severe nine-cat drag from a high-usage player
  • Positional tweener — three sources list him at three different positions
  • 49.7% shooting is unremarkable for a seven-footer taking interior shots
