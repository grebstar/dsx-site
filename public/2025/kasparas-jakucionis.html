SCOUTING REPORT

Jakučionis is the best value on the back half of this first round by board consensus. RotoWire 11th, RookieScale 12th, Rotoworld 14th, FantasyPros 16th — ten of twelve sources put him inside the top 16, and he went 20th. That is a real discount for a 19-year-old who filled every column at Illinois: 15.0 points, 5.7 rebounds and 4.7 assists with 1.6 made threes and 84.5% from the line.

The rebounding is what makes the profile: 5.7 from a guard is a top-five mark in this class and it is the kind of category contribution that does not depend on a jump shot falling.

The 3.7 turnovers is the second-worst number in the draft, behind only Danny Wolf. He plays through contact and into traffic, and 31.8% from three means defenses can go under everything and dare him. Those two problems compound.

Miami is a mixed landing spot — a well-run organization with a crowded guard rotation and a coach not known for handing rookies minutes. NBA Draft Room and Hollinger priced that in, ranking him 23rd and 18th.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Ten of twelve boards ranked him 7th-16th against a #20 draft slot — the clearest value gap on the board
  • 5.7 rebounds from a guard is a top-five mark in the class
  • 84.5% from the line at 19; the shooting indicator is strong even if the three-point rate is not
  • Fills points, rebounds, assists and threes at once with no dead category

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 3.7 turnovers — second-worst in the entire draft
  • 31.8% from three lets defenses go under screens and take the drive away
  • 0.3 blocks and 0.9 steals; the defensive categories are thin
  • Erik Spoelstra's rotation is crowded and historically unfriendly to rookies
