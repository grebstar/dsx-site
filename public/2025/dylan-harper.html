SCOUTING REPORT

Harper's Rutgers season is a genuine lead-guard profile: 19.4 points, 4.6 rebounds, 4.0 assists and 1.4 steals on 48.4% shooting, which is remarkable efficiency for a 19-year-old carrying that much creation on a bad team. He gets downhill whenever he wants, finishes through contact, and the steal rate is real rather than a gambling artifact.

The nine-cat concerns are the ones that follow every high-usage young guard: 33.3% from three on decent volume, 75.0% from the line, 2.4 turnovers. None of those are disqualifying and all three are the kind of number that typically improves. On talent alone he is the second-best asset in this draft and eleven of the twelve boards have him second.

The problem is San Antonio. He walks into a roster with De'Aaron Fox extended, Stephon Castle coming off Rookie of the Year, Devin Vassell on the wing and Victor Wembanyama absorbing everything in the frontcourt. This is the single worst year-one opportunity of any top-five pick in the class, and it is why his Pathway score sits at 58 against a model base of 94 — a 36-point analyst override, the largest downgrade on the board.

Dynasty holders should not care much. Redraft holders should care a great deal. That split is exactly why he ranks 2nd on nearly every long-horizon board and 4th to 6th on every September redraft list.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Second-best talent in the class by near-unanimous board consensus
  • 48.4% from the field at 19 while carrying primary creation duty at Rutgers
  • 1.4 steals from a big guard — the scarce category, already present
  • San Antonio's timeline means he will be handed the keys well before his rookie deal ends

▼ BEAR CASE

  • Worst year-one depth chart of any top-five pick — Fox, Castle and Vassell all ahead of him
  • 33.3% from three and 75.0% from the line both need to move
  • Two Summer League games at 35.7% before San Antonio shut him down
  • Redraft value is close to zero in year one; you are paying pick 1.02 for a year-two asset
