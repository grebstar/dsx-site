SCOUTING REPORT

Riley came off the bench for Illinois and won Big Ten Sixth Man of the Year on 12.6 points and 4.1 rebounds in 25.5 minutes. He is a long, fluid scoring wing at 19 with real shot creation, and Washington — heading for the worst record in the league — is the single most open usage situation available to any rookie in this draft.

The category profile is the problem. 0.3 steals and 0.3 blocks are the lowest combined defensive rates of any first-rounder, 2.2 assists is thin, and 32.6% from three means the scoring is not yet efficient. He fills one category well and four categories not at all.

Board coverage has one gap: RotoWire did not rank him at all, and the eleven that did placed him between 23rd and 37th. Nobody was excited. That is the correct price for a bench scorer with no defensive production, however good the landing spot.

The bet here is entirely on Washington's emptiness. If minutes are the only input that matters, Riley will get more of them than his talent alone would earn.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Washington will be the worst team in the league — nobody in the class has more available usage
  • Big Ten Sixth Man of the Year at 19; the shot creation is real
  • Long, fluid scoring wing with genuine positional size
  • 58.3% from the field and 57.1% from three in a small Summer League sample

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 0.3 steals and 0.3 blocks — the thinnest defensive production of any first-rounder
  • 32.6% from three and 43.2% from the field; scoring without efficiency
  • RotoWire left him off their board entirely; no source ranked him higher than 23rd
  • 2.2 assists means the profile is one category deep
