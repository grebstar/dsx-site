SCOUTING REPORT

1.5 steals and 1.3 blocks on 58.6% shooting. That combination — both defensive categories at volume, with elite efficiency — appears exactly once in this class, and it is why NBC called him a "category league star." Add 16.8 points and 8.3 rebounds and the South Carolina line is one of the most productive in the draft.

The reason he sits tenth rather than fifth is a single missing skill. 0.3 made threes on 26.5%. He is a 6'7" forward who cannot shoot, in a league that is increasingly unwilling to play those, and the question of whether Toronto can find 28 minutes a night for him is genuinely open. NBA Draft Room left him off their published top 30 entirely — the only lottery pick they omitted.

He is also the second-oldest player in this lottery at 20.0, which trims the developmental runway relative to the teenagers ranked around him.

What you are buying is a defensive engine with an unusually high floor of per-minute production and a real cap on how many minutes he gets. Toronto's frontcourt is crowded and Scottie Barnes occupies the position most naturally his. Eleven of twelve boards listed him; the one omission is NBA Draft Room.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 1.5 steals and 1.3 blocks together — the only player in the class supplying both at that rate
  • 58.6% from the field, one of the most efficient high-usage seasons in the draft
  • 16.8 and 8.3 as a sophomore; the raw production is top-five in the class
  • 20 pts / 9 reb / 4 steals against Golden State in Las Vegas to send Toronto to the semifinals

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 0.3 made threes on 26.5% — a non-shooter at a position that requires shooting
  • NBA Draft Room omitted him from their top 30; the other eleven boards listed him
  • Second-oldest player in the lottery at 20.0
  • Toronto's frontcourt is crowded and Scottie Barnes holds his natural position
