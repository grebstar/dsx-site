SCOUTING REPORT

Clayton is the most decorated player in this draft and the one with the least runway. He carried Florida to the 2025 national championship and was named NCAA Tournament Most Outstanding Player, a consensus first-team All-American who shot 3.0 made threes a game on 38.6% from deep and 87.5% from the line. That free-throw number is the second-best in the class behind Knueppel.

In a category format the profile is clean: threes, assists, free-throw percentage and enough steals (1.2) to matter. He is a genuine lead guard who can play off the ball, and Utah's depth chart offers nothing to keep him out of the rotation.

The whole discount is age. He is 22.3 on draft night, more than three years older than Flagg and Essengue, which is why NBA boards had him 18th and the dynasty boards split hard: NBA Draft Room ranked him 9th, RotoWire 23rd. That is a 14-place gap between those two and a 20-place spread across all twelve boards, one of the widest in the class, and it is entirely a disagreement about how to weigh proven college production against remaining development curve.

You are buying near-term production at a discount, with the ceiling capped by the same thing that makes the floor safe.

▲ BULL CASE

  • NCAA champion and Final Four Most Outstanding Player — the most proven winner in the class
  • 3.0 made threes on 38.6% from deep with 87.5% from the line; the shooting is real and durable
  • Utah's depth chart is the emptiest in the league — nothing blocks the minutes
  • NBA Draft Room ranked him 9th, well above his draft slot

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 22.3 years old — the shortest development runway of any player in the first round
  • 44.8% from the field on high usage; he is a shot-maker, not an efficient one
  • 0.5 blocks and modest rebounding — the profile is guard categories only
  • 20-place spread across the boards; nobody agrees what he is worth
