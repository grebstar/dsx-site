SCOUTING REPORT

Portland taking Yang at 16 was the loudest surprise of draft night, and the statistical case is genuinely strong: 16.2 points, 10.0 rebounds, 2.8 assists and 2.7 blocks on 59.5% shooting across 53 CBA games. Those are volume numbers in a real professional league, and the passing from a 7'1" center is the part scouts kept coming back to.

The dynasty boards were split but not dismissive — RotoWire 15th, NBA Draft Room 18th, against RookieScale 34th. That spread widens to 32 places across the twelve boards (15th at RotoWire to 47th at The Athletic), the widest of any player in the first round, and it maps almost exactly onto how much you discount Chinese Basketball Association production.

That discount should be substantial. The CBA is a considerable step below the EuroLeague, and Yang's 3.1 turnovers and 67.7% free-throw shooting are the kind of numbers that get worse, not better, when the defenders get quicker. His 28.6% from three on 0.3 makes offers no spacing.

Portland's frontcourt is crowded with Donovan Clingan and Deandre Ayton, so the year-one pathway is close to shut. This is a stash priced as one.

▲ BULL CASE

  • 2.7 blocks and 10.0 rebounds — the raw two-way production is the best of any center in the class
  • 2.8 assists from a 7'1" center; the passing is legitimately unusual
  • 59.5% from the field across 53 professional games, not a small sample
  • RotoWire ranked him 15th and NBA Draft Room 18th — the pick was not universally seen as a reach

▼ BEAR CASE

  • CBA production carries the steepest translation discount of any league in this draft
  • 3.1 turnovers and 67.7% from the line — both usually get worse against NBA athletes
  • 28.6% from three on 0.3 makes; no floor spacing at all
  • Portland already has Donovan Clingan and Deandre Ayton in the frontcourt
