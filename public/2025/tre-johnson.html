SCOUTING REPORT

Johnson is the cleanest illustration of format risk on this board. Sports Illustrated ranked him second among all rookies for 2025-26 fantasy. NBC's dynasty list ranked him thirteenth, citing "limited box-score contributions." Both are defensible, and which one you believe depends entirely on your scoring settings.

The case for: 19.9 points and 2.7 made threes per game on 39.7% from deep and 85.8% from the line, as a freshman, as SEC Freshman of the Year. He is one of the two best pure shooters in this class alongside Knueppel, and Washington is the emptiest usage situation in the NBA. Points and threes are going to be there in volume from day one.

The case against, in nine-cat terms: 3.1 rebounds, 2.7 assists, 0.9 steals, 0.3 blocks. That is four categories where he does nothing, on a 42.7% field goal percentage that actively hurts you in a fifth. He is a two-category specialist with an efficiency problem in a format that rewards breadth.

The Pathway score of 91 against a model base of 82 reflects how open the Washington depth chart is — there is nobody to keep him off the floor. The Long-Term score of 72 against a base of 76 reflects the opposite: an archetype whose value is capped unless the efficiency moves.

▲ BULL CASE

  • Washington has the emptiest usage situation in the league — volume is guaranteed
  • 2.7 made threes per game on 39.7% from deep, with 85.8% from the line backing it up
  • SEC Freshman of the Year; the shot-making is genuinely elite for the age
  • 19.5 PPG on 58.3% shooting in Summer League — the scoring translated immediately

▼ BEAR CASE

  • 3.1 rebounds, 2.7 assists, 0.9 steals, 0.3 blocks — four categories contribute nothing
  • 42.7% from the field is a real drag in a percentage format
  • NBC ranked him 13th in dynasty for exactly these reasons; the split is not noise
  • Washington will be historically bad, which caps team-context value in year one
