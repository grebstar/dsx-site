SCOUTING REPORT

Beringer is the youngest big in the class and the rawest player in the first round. He picked up basketball at 13, and by 18 he was starting in the Adriatic League and EuroCup for Cedevita Olimpija. The line is small — 4.8 points and 4.1 rebounds in 17.8 minutes — but the rate that matters is 1.4 blocks, and he shot 60.4% from the field because he only takes shots he cannot miss.

Six of the twelve boards liked him more than his draft slot: RotoWire 14th, NBA Draft Room 15th, RookieScale 16th, all against a #17 pick. That is unusual for a player this unfinished, and it reflects how much a category format values a young shot-blocker with a clean efficiency profile.

What he cannot do is long. Zero three-point attempts across every competition he played in 2024-25, 0.6 assists, and 55.8% from the free-throw line — that last number is a genuine nine-cat liability, not a rounding error. He is a lob finisher and a rim protector, and nothing else yet.

Minnesota is the wrong landing spot for year one and the right one for year three: Rudy Gobert occupies exactly his position, but the Wolves can afford to develop him slowly. The Pathway score of 46 against a model base of 64 prices that honestly.
