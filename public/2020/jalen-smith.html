SCOUTING REPORT

Phoenix took him tenth overall out of Maryland. He had just gone for 15.5 points, 10.5 rebounds and 0.8 assists in 31 games at 31.2 minutes a night. He went 10 picks ahead of his composite, which was #20. The boards ranged from #15 to #30 on him, a spread of 15 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 27 games, 1 starts, 5.8 minutes — 2.0 / 1.4 / 0.1 on 44.0% shooting. That ranked him #45 in the class and marked him at $3.76, -93.4% against his IPO. 2025-26: 53 games, 21 starts, 20.7 minutes — 10.2 / 6.7 / 1.2 on 48.3% shooting. That ranked him #17 in the class and marked him at $75.99, +33.5% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $56.94, today's mark of $75.99 is +33.5%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay.

Phoenix took him tenth and then declined his third-year rookie option on Nov. 1, 2021, the league deadline for that decision, which made him a 2022 unrestricted free agent while he was still on the roster. Three months later, on Feb. 10, 2022, the Suns traded him: Indiana received Smith, a 2022 Phoenix second-round pick and a trade exception; Phoenix received Torrey Craig and cash. He re-signed with Indiana that July, declined a player option in 2024 and signed with Chicago on a three-year, $27,000,000 contract via the non-taxpayer mid-level exception, fully guaranteed, at $9,428,571 for 2026-27 with no options.

2025-26 gave him 21 starts in 53 games and then ended early. He started 21 of 53 games at 20.7 minutes, averaging 12.1 points and 7.1 rebounds across those starts, before aggravating a right calf issue against Philadelphia; Chicago ruled him out for the final two-and-a-half weeks. The Bulls went 31-51, added Nic Claxton in the offseason, and he is projected to open 2026-27 as the backup center.
