SCOUTING REPORT

Miami took him #20 overall out of Memphis. He had just gone for 15.8 points, 10.8 rebounds and 1.0 assists in 31 games at 30.4 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #19, pick 20. The boards ranged from #14 to #29 on him, a spread of 15 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 61 games, 4 starts, 12.1 minutes — 5.0 / 3.4 / 0.5 on 54.4% shooting. That ranked him #26 in the class and marked him at $33.31, -46.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 73 games, 57 starts, 23.9 minutes — 10.1 / 6.7 / 1.4 on 52.8% shooting. That ranked him #14 in the class and marked him at $104.27, +66.6% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $62.59, today's mark of $104.27 is +66.6%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay.

Miami made the pick and kept it for a season. On Aug. 6, 2021 Toronto received Achiuwa, Goran Dragic and a trade exception, and Miami received Kyle Lowry. Two and a half years later, on Dec. 30, 2023, New York received Achiuwa, OG Anunoby, Malachi Flynn and a trade exception, and Toronto received R.J. Barrett, Immanuel Quickley and a 2024 Detroit second-round pick -- a deal that moved three members of this class at once.

The last two years have been a sequence of short contracts. He reached free agency in three consecutive summers. Miami signed him on Sep. 25, 2025 and waived him on Oct. 17, before the regular season began; Sacramento signed him on Nov. 4 and he started 57 of 73 games there at 23.9 minutes for 10.1 points and 6.7 rebounds on .528 shooting, all above his career marks entering the season. He hit free agency again on Jul. 1, 2026 and Sacramento brought him back within a week on a multi-year deal reported at two years and $11.5 million. He was a Rising Stars selection in each of his first two seasons.
