SCOUTING REPORT

Portland took him #16 overall out of Washington. He had just gone for 17.0 points, 8.8 rebounds and 0.8 assists in 32 games at 32.3 minutes a night. He went 14 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #30, drafted 16. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #22 to #46 on him, a spread of 24 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 68 games, 14 starts, 21.4 minutes — 7.9 / 6.7 / 0.9 on 55.3% shooting. That ranked him seventh in the class and marked him at $269.91, +1054.1% against his IPO. 2025-26: 58 games, 13 starts, 22.7 minutes — 10.0 / 5.0 / 1.1 on 55.0% shooting. That ranked him #18 in the class and marked him at $68.95, +194.8% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $23.39, today's mark of $68.95 is +194.8%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished seventh in the All-Rookie vote among 2020 draftees.

The slot reads Portland because Portland made the pick, and his rights moved twice before he ever signed. Houston received the rights to Stewart, Trevor Ariza and a conditional 2021 first-round pick from Portland, and Portland received Robert Covington. Then Detroit received the rights to Stewart, Trevor Ariza, a future second-round pick and cash from Houston, and Houston received Christian Wood, a protected future first-round pick and a 2021 second-round pick. The two ledgers log those transfers on different days -- one treats both as draft-night business on Nov. 18, the other dates them Nov. 22 and Nov. 24 -- so no date is asserted, and one names the second-rounder as a 2027 pick where the other says only 'future'.

He signed a four-year extension with Detroit on Jul. 11, 2023, reported at $64 million; what remains on it is described as $15 million plus a $15 million team option in one place and as $18,121,080 in total in another, so no figure is stated. 2025-26 recovered his production -- 10.0 points on .550 shooting with 1.6 blocks in 22.7 minutes across 58 games, after 6.0 a game the year before -- for a Detroit team that went 60-22, beat Orlando 4-3 and lost the conference semifinal to Cleveland 4-3. A left calf strain cost him at least four games in March. In July 2026 Detroit moved him to Memphis in a six-team trade described as a salary-clearing move.
