SCOUTING REPORT

Boston took him #26 overall out of Oregon. He had just gone for 20.5 points, 4.3 rebounds and 5.5 assists in 31 games at 36.5 minutes a night. He went 12 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #38, drafted 26. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #27 to #60 on him, a spread of 33 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 66 games, 4 starts, 19.2 minutes — 7.7 / 2.4 / 1.8 on 44.0% shooting. That ranked him twelfth in the class and marked him at $131.64, +1137.4% against his IPO. 2025-26: 79 games, 50 starts, 32.4 minutes — 17.0 / 3.9 / 5.2 on 46.4% shooting. That ranked him eighth in the class and marked him at $229.10, +2053.4% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $10.64, today's mark of $229.10 is +2053.4%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished twelfth in the All-Rookie vote among 2020 draftees.

No trade has ever moved him. Boston made the pick, exercised two team options and signed him to a rookie-scale extension in October 2023 with two years still to run as of August 2026 and unrestricted free agency listed for 2028; Boston can add three further years at higher pay if both sides agree terms in October. He said in August that he was not really looking at an extension. The dollar figures are reported two ways -- a four-year, $30 million deal in 2023 against a $67 million figure attached to a prospective new one -- so neither appears here.

He is a 2024 NBA champion and won Sixth Man of the Year for 2024-25, not for the season on this page. 2025-26 was the year the bench role became a starting one: 79 games with 50 starts at 32.4 minutes for 17.0 points, 5.2 assists and .890 from the line, after 3 starts the year before. Boston finished 56-26 and lost the first round to Philadelphia 3-4. With Jaylen Brown traded that summer, his role is expected to widen again beside Jayson Tatum; a ten-game sample without Brown produced 25.2 points on .485 shooting with 7.0 assists.
