SCOUTING REPORT

Cleveland took him fifth overall out of Auburn. He had just gone for 12.8 points, 4.4 rebounds and 2.0 assists in 28 games at 31.5 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #8, pick 5. The boards ranged from fifth to eleventh on him, a spread of 6 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 67 games, 67 starts, 32.4 minutes — 9.6 / 3.1 / 1.9 on 42.0% shooting. That ranked him eighth in the class and marked him at $229.10, +0.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: 63 games, 62 starts, 26.9 minutes — 9.3 / 2.7 / 1.6 on 46.0% shooting. That ranked him #21 in the class and marked him at $51.92, -77.3% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $229.10, today's mark of $51.92 is -77.3%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished eighth in the All-Rookie vote among 2020 draftees.

Cleveland made the pick and kept it, and he started all 67 games he played as a rookie. The one trade of his career came on Jul. 6, 2025: Cleveland received Lonzo Ball and Chicago received Okoro, who had two years left on his contract. One ledger adds a trade exception to Cleveland's side of that deal and the news account names only the two players, so the exception is not asserted here.

The contract is the three-year deal he signed with Cleveland on Sep. 17, 2024 as a restricted free agent, reported at three years and $38MM with $33MM guaranteed and bonuses tied to team success; it transferred to Chicago in the trade and runs through 2026-27. Chicago used him as its starting perimeter defender: 62 starts in 63 games at 26.9 minutes, up from 22 starts and 19.1 minutes in his last Cleveland season. He missed the Feb. 11 game against Boston with a sore left knee and several games in the season's final week with a quadriceps injury. The Bulls finished 31-51.
