SCOUTING REPORT

Oklahoma City took him #25 overall out of Kentucky. He had just gone for 16.1 points, 4.2 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 30 games at 32.9 minutes a night. He went 15 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #40, drafted 25. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #34 to #60 on him, a spread of 26 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 64 games, 3 starts, 19.4 minutes — 11.4 / 2.1 / 2.0 on 39.5% shooting. That ranked him fifth in the class and marked him at $396.43, +4670.3% against his IPO. 2025-26: 70 games, 70 starts, 31.9 minutes — 16.4 / 4.0 / 5.9 on 44.3% shooting. That ranked him seventh in the class and marked him at $269.91, +3147.8% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $8.31, today's mark of $269.91 is +3147.8%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished fifth in the All-Rookie vote among 2020 draftees.

The slot reads Oklahoma City and he never played there. In the three-team deal of Nov. 20, 2020, New York received the rights to Quickley from Oklahoma City along with Mathias Lessort and a 2023 second-round pick from Minnesota; Minnesota received Ricky Rubio and the rights to Jaden McDaniels and Leandro Bolmaro; and Oklahoma City received the rights to Aleksej Pokusevski, James Johnson and a 2024 Minnesota second-round pick. Whether his rights passed through Minnesota is itself reported two ways and is not asserted here.

On Dec. 30, 2023 Toronto received Quickley, R.J. Barrett and a 2024 Detroit second-round pick, and New York received OG Anunoby, Precious Achiuwa and Malachi Flynn -- three members of this class in one transaction. Toronto re-signed him in 2024 on a five-year deal reported at $175MM in one account and as worth up to $175 million with incentives in another, with the signing year given as both 2023 and 2024; no figure or year appears here. 2025-26 answered the availability question -- 70 games, all starts, 16.4 points and 5.9 assists, after 33 games the year before -- and then reopened it. He missed time late with right-foot plantar fasciitis, strained his right hamstring in the regular-season finale against Brooklyn, aggravated it working back, and sat out the rest of a first-round series Cleveland won 4-3, with Jamal Shead starting in his place.
