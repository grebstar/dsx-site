SCOUTING REPORT

San Antonio took him eleventh overall out of Florida State. He had just gone for 12.7 points, 5.1 rebounds and 1.6 assists in 30 games at 28.7 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #10, pick 11. The boards ranged from seventh to #13 on him, a spread of 6 places across 10 of them.

2020-21: 62 games, 7 starts, 17.0 minutes — 5.5 / 2.8 / 0.9 on 40.6% shooting. That ranked him #19 in the class and marked him at $62.59, -63.3% against his IPO. 2025-26: 67 games, 65 starts, 30.5 minutes — 13.9 / 4.0 / 2.5 on 43.7% shooting. That ranked him #13 in the class and marked him at $116.93, -31.5% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $170.70, today's mark of $116.93 is -31.5%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished #19 in the All-Rookie vote among 2020 draftees.

No trade has ever moved him. San Antonio's ledger holds the draft selection, the rookie contract, two team-option exercises and the rookie-scale extension signed on Oct. 3, 2023, and nothing else. That extension runs five years from 2024-25 through 2028-29; the total is reported at $146 million in one place and at $135,000,000 plus $11,000,000 in unlikely-to-earn incentives in another, so no figure appears here. He missed the start of 2024-25 after foot surgery.

2025-26 was a season of doing less on a team that did much more. He started 65 of 67 games at 30.5 minutes for a 62-20 San Antonio side that reached the NBA Finals, and his scoring fell for the second straight year, from 19.5 to 16.3 to 13.9, on fewer attempts than in any season since Victor Wembanyama arrived. The three-point rate held at .384. An adductor strain cost him a reported thirteen games. He raised his output in the postseason, averaging 12.6 points and 5.8 rebounds against Portland and 18.3 points, 5.7 rebounds and 1.7 steals through the first two games of the conference finals; New York took the Finals 4-1.
