SCOUTING REPORT

Niang's last college season at Iowa State was a 20.5 point, 6.2 rebound, 3.3 assist year in 33.2 minutes over 35 games, with 54.6% shooting, 1.6 made threes a game and 80.7% at the line. He was a consensus second-team All-American, won the Karl Malone Award and made first-team All-Big 12 for the second straight year, finishing a four-year career of 138 games.

Indiana took him 50th overall on June 23, 2016, with no draft-night trade. Two of the seven boards ranked him, at 30th and 54th; the other five left him off, and his composite rank was 49th. He played five Orlando Summer League games at 10.2 points, 6.2 rebounds and 2.6 assists.

His rookie year was 23 games at 4.0 minutes, plus six G League games for Fort Wayne. Waived by Indiana and then by Golden State after camp, he signed a two-way deal with Utah in January 2018, played 9 Jazz games and made All-NBA G League First Team. On a standard Utah contract he played 59 games in 2018-19 (41.0% from three), 66 in 2019-20 and 72 with 10 starts in 2020-21 (42.5% from three). In Philadelphia he played 76 and 78 games at 9.2 and 8.2 points, both seasons over 40% from three, and in Cleveland he played all 82 in 2023-24 at 9.4 points. Cleveland traded him to Atlanta on Feb. 6, 2025; that season he played 79 games with 3 starts at 21.5 minutes, 9.9 points and 40.6% from three, including 12.1 points in 28 games as a Hawk. He was traded to Boston in July 2025 and to Utah in August, then missed all of 2025-26 with a left foot stress reaction before being traded to Memphis and waived in February 2026.

His contracts: a three-year rookie deal with Indiana ($750,000 guaranteed), a two-way with Utah, a three-year, $4.98 million Utah deal in July 2018, two years and $6.765 million with Philadelphia in August 2021, and three years and $25.5 million with Cleveland in July 2023. On Aug. 25, 2026 he signed a one-year minimum with Golden State at $3,876,529, with $1,838,009 guaranteed until Jan. 7 and Exhibit 3 protection tied to his left foot.

He has played all five of Golden State's games in 2026-27 at 19.4 minutes and 6.2 points, shooting 4-for-21 from three, and started on Oct. 28 at Minnesota with Gui Santos out. In camp the Warriors also used him as a small-ball center in scrimmages.
