SCOUTING REPORT

Brooklyn took him #22 overall out of Duke. He had just gone for 17.1 points, 9.9 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 36 games at 34.7 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #21, pick 22. The boards ranged from #14 to #60 on him, a spread of 46 places across 16 of them.

2013-14: 70 games, 22 starts, 18.2 minutes — 7.4 / 4.4 / 0.9 on 65.9% shooting. That ranked him fourth in the class and marked him at $495.29, +853.9% against his IPO. 2025-26: 20 games, 3 starts, 8.6 minutes — 1.6 / 2.7 / 1.0 on 78.6% shooting. That ranked him ninth in the class and marked him at $196.79, +279.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $51.92, today's mark of $196.79 is +279.0%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished fourth in the All-Rookie vote among 2013 draftees.

Brooklyn took him 22nd. On Jun. 26, 2015 the Nets sent him to Portland with the rights to Pat Connaughton for Steve Blake and the rights to Rondae Hollis-Jefferson. Portland traded him to Denver on Feb. 13, 2017 for Jusuf Nurkic and a 2017 first-round pick (Memphis's), and Denver re-signed him to a multi-year deal on Sep. 20, 2017 after tendering a qualifying offer.

He signed with Detroit on Dec. 1, 2020; the Pistons sent him and the rights to J.T. Thor (the 37th pick) to Charlotte on Aug. 6, 2021 for the rights to Balsa Koprivica (the 57th). Charlotte traded him to the Clippers on Feb. 9, 2023 for Reggie Jackson, a 2028 second and cash. He re-signed with the Clippers on Jul. 6, 2023, signed with Phoenix on Jul. 3, 2024 and returned to Charlotte on Jul. 13, 2025 on a one-year, $3.6 million deal. On Feb. 4, 2026 the Hornets traded him to Oklahoma City for Ousmane Dieng and a 2029 second; the Thunder waived him the same day, and he finished the season with San Antonio. He became a free agent on Jul. 1, 2026.
