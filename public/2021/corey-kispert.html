SCOUTING REPORT

Washington took him #15 overall out of Gonzaga. He had just gone for 18.6 points, 5.0 rebounds and 1.8 assists in 32 games at 31.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #13, pick 15. The boards ranged from #13 to #15 on him, a spread of 2 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 77 games, 36 starts, 23.4 minutes — 8.2 / 2.7 / 1.1 on 45.5% shooting. That ranked him #17 in the class and marked him at $75.99, -35.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: 58 games, 10 starts, 18.6 minutes — 9.2 / 2.3 / 1.6 on 46.7% shooting. That ranked him #28 in the class and marked him at $27.93, -76.1% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $116.93, today's mark of $27.93 is -76.1%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

He was traded once, on Jan. 9, 2026, and it was the deal that moved a franchise point guard: Atlanta received C.J. McCollum and Kispert (RealGM adds a trade exception); Washington received Trae Young. NBA.com's tracker lists no picks and no cash either way. He signed a rookie-scale extension with Washington on Oct. 21, 2024 beginning in 2025-26; it is reported as four years and $54 million by several outlets and as three years and $34.55 million by RotoWire, so the page states no term and no figure.

Both of the last two seasons have been cut into. A torn ligament in his left thumb needed surgery in March 2025 and ended that year at 61 games; thumb and hamstring trouble — a left hamstring listed as injury management on Jan. 7, 2026 — held him to 19 Washington games before the trade. He shot .395 from three in those 19 and .354 in 39 games for Atlanta, 18.6 minutes a night across the 58. The Hawks reached the playoffs and lost the first round to New York in six, closed out on Apr. 30, 2026; it was his first postseason after four seasons in Washington. He told the Spokesman-Review in August that he and Quin Snyder see the game the same way.
