Oklahoma City took him #18 overall out of Florida. He had just gone for 16.0 points, 5.6 rebounds and 3.5 assists in 24 games at 32.4 minutes a night. He went 6 picks ahead of his composite, which was #24. The boards ranged from #16 to #28 on him, a spread of 12 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 60 games, 26 starts, 22.8 minutes — 10.4 / 2.9 / 1.5 on 39.3% shooting. That ranked him #14 in the class and marked him at $104.27, +162.6% against his IPO. 2025-26: 53 games, 1 starts, 12.6 minutes — 5.5 / 1.7 / 1.6 on 36.0% shooting. That ranked him #31 in the class and marked him at $21.38, -46.1% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $39.70, today's mark of $21.38 is -46.1%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

Two trades, both as salary and depth. On Feb. 8, 2024, Charlotte received Mann, Davis Bertans, Vasilije Micic, Houston's own 2024 second-round pick, Philadelphia's own 2025 second-round pick, cash and a trade exception; Oklahoma City received Gordon Hayward. In August 2026, inside the multi-team Peyton Watson deal, Washington received Mann, a 2027 second-round pick that reached the Clippers by way of Cleveland and originated with Philadelphia, and cash; Washington sent out Cam Whitmore. Charlotte's own leg brought back Dennis Schroder and cash.

A disc herniation in his back limited him to 13 games in 2024-25; he was declared fully healthy in June 2025 and re-signed with Charlotte on Jul. 13, 2025. The terms are reported three ways — three years and $24 million, two years and $16 million with a team option, and simply a three-year extension — so none appears here. 2025-26 was a shrinking season: 53 games, one start, 12.6 minutes, 5.5 points on .360 and .323. Charlotte went 44-38 and lost a play-in game to Orlando 121-90 on Apr. 17, 2026. He has not appeared in an NBA playoff game.
