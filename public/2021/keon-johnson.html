SCOUTING REPORT

New York took him #21 overall out of Tennessee. He had just gone for 11.3 points, 3.5 rebounds and 2.5 assists in 27 games at 25.5 minutes a night. The boards had him 10 places above where he went, at #11. The boards ranged from sixth to #15 on him, a spread of 9 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 37 games, 12 starts, 18.8 minutes — 7.2 / 2.2 / 2.1 on 35.3% shooting. That ranked him #23 in the class and marked him at $43.38, -71.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: he did not appear in an NBA game. Maine Celtics — G League.

Against a frozen IPO of $149.42, today's mark of $8.31 is -94.4%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

He never played for New York, and the pick that replaced him is on this same board. On draft night the Clippers received the No. 21 selection, which became Keon Johnson; New York received the No. 25 pick, which became Quentin Grimes, plus a future second-round pick that was Detroit's 2024 selection. On Feb. 4, 2022, Portland received Eric Bledsoe, Justise Winslow, Keon Johnson and a 2025 second-round pick via Detroit; the Clippers received Norman Powell and Robert Covington. He moved a third time in the September 2023 three-team Damian Lillard deal, going from Portland to Phoenix alongside Jusuf Nurkic and Nassir Little while Milwaukee took Lillard and Portland took Jrue Holiday, Deandre Ayton, Toumani Camara and Milwaukee draft compensation.

He is not an injury case in 2025-26. Brooklyn exercised his $2,349,578 team option on Jun. 28, 2025, then waived him on Sep. 17 against $271,614 guaranteed; he cleared on Sep. 19 and went unsigned for two and a half months. The Maine Celtics claimed him off G League waivers on Dec. 1, 2025, and he played 16 games there from Dec. 6 to Mar. 31 at 24.5 minutes for 15.9 points on .393 and .279, including 43 points on 11-of-18 with 12-of-12 free throws on Mar. 16, 2026. The season before, he had started 56 of 79 games for Brooklyn at 24.4 minutes.
