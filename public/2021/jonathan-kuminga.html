SCOUTING REPORT

Golden State took him seventh overall out of G League Ignite in the NBA G League. He had just gone for 15.8 points, 7.2 rebounds and 2.7 assists in 13 games at 32.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #5, pick 7. The boards ranged from fifth to seventh on him, a spread of 2 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 70 games, 12 starts, 16.9 minutes — 9.3 / 3.3 / 0.9 on 51.3% shooting. That ranked him #16 in the class and marked him at $84.15, -78.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 36 games, 14 starts, 23.1 minutes — 12.2 / 5.6 / 2.3 on 46.3% shooting. That ranked him #21 in the class and marked him at $51.92, -86.9% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $396.43, today's mark of $51.92 is -86.9%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

He asked out, and in February 2026 Golden State obliged. Atlanta received Kuminga and Buddy Hield; Golden State received Kristaps Porzingis. Hoops Rumors describes it as a straight player exchange with no draft picks either way — Porzingis on a $30.7 million expiring deal going west, Kuminga at $22.5 million with a $24.3 million team option and Hield at $9.7 million going east. He won a title as an 18-year-old rookie in 2022, having played 70 games at 16.9 minutes with G League assignments to Santa Cruz.

A left knee bone bruise carried over from his final Golden State weeks delayed his Atlanta debut through the All-Star break and six Hawks games; when he did play, on Feb. 25, 2026, he came off the bench for 27 points on 9-of-12 with seven rebounds in a 119-98 win over Washington. Knee inflammation cost him more later. Atlanta lost the first round to New York 4-2 with Kuminga off the bench for all six, shooting .208 from three. The Hawks let him reach free agency on Jul. 1, 2026 and he signed a two-year, $13 million deal with a player option with Minnesota on Aug. 26, 2026, having reportedly sought closer to $20 million a year.
