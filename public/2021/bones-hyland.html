SCOUTING REPORT

Denver took him #26 overall out of VCU. He had just gone for 19.5 points, 4.7 rebounds and 2.1 assists in 24 games at 31.9 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #29, pick 26. The boards ranged from #25 to #38 on him, a spread of 13 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 69 games, 4 starts, 19.0 minutes — 10.1 / 2.7 / 2.8 on 40.3% shooting. That ranked him eighth in the class and marked him at $229.10, +795.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 71 games, 3 starts, 16.6 minutes — 8.5 / 1.8 / 2.6 on 45.3% shooting. That ranked him #25 in the class and marked him at $36.38, +42.3% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $25.57, today's mark of $36.38 is +42.3%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished eighth in the All-Rookie vote among 2021 draftees.

Two trades and a waiver. On Feb. 9, 2023, the Clippers received Hyland; Denver received the Clippers' 2024 and 2025 second-round picks. On Feb. 6, 2025, Atlanta received Terance Mann and Hyland; the Clippers received Bogdan Bogdanovic and three second-round picks. Atlanta waived him two days later.

Minnesota picked him up on a two-way on Feb. 27, 2025, converted him to a standard contract on Sep. 15, 2025, and he played 71 games in 2025-26 as the backup point guard: 16.6 minutes, 8.5 points, 2.6 assists, on .453 from the field and .388 from three, both above his rookie numbers, with six games of 20 or more points. Minnesota went 49-33, beat Denver 4-2 in the first round and lost the conference semifinals to San Antonio 2-4. He played all ten playoff games at 11.6 minutes and shot .300 from the field and .265 from three in them, with three scoreless appearances against San Antonio. He re-signed with Minnesota on a one-year minimum contract in the summer of 2026 — reported Jun. 30 by Sports Illustrated, Jul. 1 by Yahoo, and logged Jul. 3 by RealGM.
