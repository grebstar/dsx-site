SCOUTING REPORT

Toronto took him fourth overall out of Florida State. He had just gone for 10.3 points, 4.0 rebounds and 4.1 assists in 24 games at 24.7 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #6, pick 4. The boards ranged from fifth to #14 on him, a spread of 9 places across 5 of them.

2021-22: 74 games, 74 starts, 35.4 minutes — 15.3 / 7.5 / 3.5 on 49.2% shooting. That ranked him first in the class and marked him at $1,000.00, +209.7% against his IPO. 2025-26: 80 games, 80 starts, 33.5 minutes — 18.1 / 7.5 / 5.9 on 50.7% shooting. That ranked him fourth in the class and marked him at $495.29, +53.4% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $322.90, today's mark of $495.29 is +53.4%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished first in the All-Rookie vote among 2021 draftees.

He has never been traded, and he has the availability the top of this class mostly does not: 80 games and 80 starts in 2025-26, plus all seven playoff games. He signed a five-year rookie-scale maximum extension in July 2024, reported at $224.9 million by one outlet and at up to $270 million by others, so no figure appears here.

2025-26 brought his first All-Defensive selection, on the Second Team with 130 points, and a fifth-place finish in Defensive Player of the Year voting on three second-place votes and twelve thirds, 21 points in all. He said publicly that he thought he belonged on the First Team. Toronto went 46-36 and took the fifth seed. In the first round against Cleveland he averaged 24.1 points, 6.1 rebounds and 8.6 assists in 39.0 minutes on .509 from the field and .381 from three, playing through a quad contusion from a Thomas Bryant knee and an eye poke in Game 5 — afterwards he said it only hurt that one game. Toronto lost Game 7 in Cleveland 114-102.
