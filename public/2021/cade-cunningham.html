Detroit took him first overall out of Oklahoma State. He had just gone for 20.1 points, 6.2 rebounds and 3.5 assists in 27 games at 35.4 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #1, pick 1. All 5 boards had him at first. There was no disagreement to price.

That ranked him first in the class and marked him at $1,000.00, +0.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $1,000.00, today's mark of $1,000.00 is +0.0%. The board reads that as MARKET GOT IT RIGHT. Holding the top rung against a whole class trying to take it is the hardest outcome available here, not a neutral one. He finished second in the All-Rookie vote among 2021 draftees. He made All-NBA First Team in 2025-26 with 414 vote points, which is why he sits where he does at that mark.

He has never been traded. The rookie year opened late — a right ankle sprain in training camp cost him the whole preseason and the Oct. 20, 2021 opener against Chicago, with Dwane Casey declining to set a timetable — and Detroit assigned him to the Motor City Cruise for three days that October before recalling him. He signed a five-year rookie-scale maximum extension in July 2024. The reported value is not stated here: one account puts it at $224 million, another at $269,085,780 after the All-NBA escalator, and the page will not pick between them.

The 2025-26 interruption was strange and serious. On March 17, 2026, diving for a loose ball against Washington, he collided with Tre Johnson and suffered a pneumothorax — a collapsed left lung. Detroit announced it two days later and he was still being re-evaluated a week at a time on April 2 with six games to play. He came back for the postseason and played all 14 of them at 40.9 minutes a night. Detroit was 60-22 and the No. 1 seed; it beat Orlando 4-3 after trailing, then went out in the conference semifinals to Cleveland in seven, losing Game 7 at home 125-94 having once been up 2-0.
