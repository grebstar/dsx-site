SCOUTING REPORT

Milwaukee took him #17 overall out of Villanova. He had just gone for 13.4 points, 4.8 rebounds and 3.5 assists in 40 games at 29.3 minutes a night. He went 12 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #29, drafted 17. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #18 to #60 on him, a spread of 42 places across 6 of them.

2018-19: 27 games, 0 starts, 15.2 minutes — 4.9 / 2.4 / 1.1 on 40.3% shooting. That ranked him #29 in the class and marked him at $25.57, +0.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: 82 games, 82 starts, 30.4 minutes — 12.2 / 4.1 / 3.8 on 40.6% shooting. That ranked him ninth in the class and marked him at $196.79, +669.5% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $25.57, today's mark of $196.79 is +669.5%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay.

Milwaukee took him seventeenth on Jun. 21, 2018. On Feb. 10, 2022 he went to Sacramento in a four-team deal: the Kings received him, Trey Lyles and Josh Jackson, Detroit received Marvin Bagley III, Milwaukee received Serge Ibaka and the Clippers received Rodney Hood and Semi Ojeleye. Golden State and New York followed as free-agent signings, and in September 2024 the Knicks traded him, Julius Randle, Keita Bates-Diop and a future first to Minnesota for Karl-Anthony Towns.

2025-26 was all 82 games as a starter at 12.2 / 4.1 / 3.8 on .379 from three. Then, early in Game 4 of Minnesota's second-round series against Denver in late April, he tore his right Achilles tendon. Minnesota won that series and lost the conference semifinals to San Antonio 4-2. He is on the four-year contract he signed with New York in 2023, now carried by the Timberwolves.
