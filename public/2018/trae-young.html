SCOUTING REPORT

Dallas took him fifth overall out of Oklahoma. He had just gone for 27.4 points, 3.9 rebounds and 8.7 assists in 32 games at 35.4 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #8, pick 5. The boards ranged from third to #25 on him, a spread of 22 places across 6 of them.

2018-19: 81 games, 81 starts, 30.9 minutes — 19.1 / 3.7 / 8.1 on 41.8% shooting. That ranked him second in the class and marked him at $808.24, +252.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 15 games, 15 starts, 25.6 minutes — 17.9 / 2.0 / 8.0 on 45.8% shooting. That ranked him #13 in the class and marked him at $116.93, -49.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $229.10, today's mark of $116.93 is -49.0%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished second in the All-Rookie vote among 2018 draftees.

Dallas drafted him fifth on Jun. 21, 2018 and sent him to Atlanta that night for the third pick, which became Luka Doncic, plus a protected 2019 first. He then spent seven and a half seasons in Atlanta and drew the same 200 All-Rookie voting points as the man he was traded for.

On Jan. 9, 2026 the Hawks traded him to Washington for CJ McCollum and Corey Kispert. His season was 15 games across the two teams at 17.9 / 8.0: a right quad contusion and a right knee MCL sprain, later joined by a back problem, ended it, with the Wizards saying neither required surgery. He declined his player option in the summer and re-signed with Washington on a four-year contract reported at $212 million with a player option in the last year, a figure ESPN, Yahoo and NBA.com state identically. He was a four-time All-Star before the trade.
