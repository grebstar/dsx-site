SCOUTING REPORT

Orlando took him first overall out of Duke. He had just gone for 17.2 points, 7.8 rebounds and 3.2 assists in 39 games at 33.0 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #3, pick 1. The boards ranged from second to third on him, a spread of 1 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 72 games, 72 starts, 33.8 minutes — 20.0 / 6.9 / 3.7 on 42.7% shooting. Against a frozen IPO of $623.16, today's mark of $623.16 is +0.0%. The board reads that as MARKET GOT IT RIGHT — he is priced about where the consensus put him. He finished first in the All-Rookie vote among 2022 draftees.

Orlando has never asked him to be anything but the whole offense, and it has cost him availability twice. A torn right oblique reported on Oct. 31, 2024 — days after a 50-point game against Indiana — carried a four-to-six week re-evaluation and took 34 games. A left groin strain suffered against the Knicks on Nov. 12, 2025 took 10 more; on Dec. 1 his coach said he was not ready to take contact.

The money arrived first. On July 7, 2025 he agreed a five-year maximum rookie extension with a $239 million base, worth up to $287 million with All-NBA, All-Defensive, MVP and DPOY escalators, and a player option in 2030-31. What followed was the 2026 first round: Orlando led Detroit 3-1, lost three straight, and Banchero scored 38 on 14-of-25 with nine rebounds and six assists in 42 minutes of a 116-94 Game 7 defeat. He said the same day that he could not say the roster was good enough, and Orlando dismissed head coach Jamahl Mosley hours later. Sean Sweeney has the job now; Banchero says the new staff worked him out in Seattle and that he is aiming at 25 / 8 / 7.
