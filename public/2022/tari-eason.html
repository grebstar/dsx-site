SCOUTING REPORT

Houston took him #17 overall out of LSU. He had just gone for 16.9 points, 6.6 rebounds and 1.0 assists in 33 games at 24.4 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #13, pick 17. The boards ranged from eighth to #22 on him, a spread of 14 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 82 games, 5 starts, 21.5 minutes — 9.3 / 6.0 / 1.1 on 44.8% shooting. That ranked him tenth in the class and marked him at $170.70, +46.0% against his IPO. 2025-26: 60 games, 34 starts, 25.8 minutes — 10.5 / 6.3 / 1.5 on 41.6% shooting. That ranked him #17 in the class and marked him at $75.99, -35.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $116.93, today's mark of $75.99 is -35.0%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished tenth in the All-Rookie vote among 2022 draftees.

He has never been traded. What he has had is a left leg that keeps taking seasons off him. In 2023-24 Houston found a benign growth on the inside of his heel and tibia that had caused a stress reaction; the surgery ended his season with a roughly four-month timeline. An oblique strain against Portland on Nov. 14, 2025 carried a four-to-six week estimate, and the return was pushed back again by soreness in the same left shin. He has missed at least 20 games in each of two seasons to problems related to it.

He and Houston could not agree a rookie-scale extension before the Oct. 20, 2025 deadline, which put him among a dozen 2022 first-rounders headed for restricted free agency. He got there and stayed: five years, $81 million in July 2026, $16.3 million a year, after some around the league had wondered whether he would clear $20 million on the open market. He was a steady bench defender in 2025-26 at 10.5 / 6.3 with 1.5 steals.
