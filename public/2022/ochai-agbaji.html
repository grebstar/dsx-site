SCOUTING REPORT

Cleveland took him #14 overall out of Kansas. He had just gone for 18.8 points, 5.1 rebounds and 1.6 assists in 39 games at 35.0 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #16, pick 14. The boards ranged from #13 to #29 on him, a spread of 16 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 59 games, 22 starts, 20.5 minutes — 7.9 / 2.1 / 1.1 on 42.7% shooting. That ranked him #20 in the class and marked him at $56.94, -32.3% against his IPO. 2025-26: 62 games, 13 starts, 15.7 minutes — 5.1 / 2.3 / 0.8 on 43.6% shooting. That ranked him #32 in the class and marked him at $19.52, -76.8% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $84.15, today's mark of $19.52 is -76.8%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

He has been moved three times, and the first came before he played a game for the team that drafted him. On Sept. 1, 2022 Cleveland sent Collin Sexton, Lauri Markkanen, Agbaji, unprotected 2025, 2027 and 2029 first-round picks and 2026 and 2028 swaps to Utah, and received Donovan Mitchell. On Feb. 8, 2024 Utah sent Kelly Olynyk and Agbaji to Toronto for Otto Porter Jr., Kira Lewis Jr. and a 2024 first-round pick.

Toronto is where he was best — 64 games in 2024-25 at 10.4 points on 49.8% shooting and 39.9% from three — and where the extension talks failed, leaving him to play out the final year at $6.38 million. On Feb. 5, 2026 a three-team deal sent him on again: Toronto sent Agbaji and a 2032 second and received Chris Paul; Brooklyn received Agbaji, the 2032 second and $3.5 million and sent the draft rights to Vanja Marinkovic; the Clippers sent Chris Paul and $3.5 million and took those rights.

He split 2025-26 at 15.5 minutes across 42 Toronto games and 16.2 across 20 in Brooklyn. On June 29, 2026 Brooklyn declined his qualifying offer and he became an unrestricted free agent at 26. No signing had been reported.
