SCOUTING REPORT

Oklahoma City took him second overall out of Gonzaga. He had just gone for 14.1 points, 9.9 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 32 games at 26.9 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #2, pick 2. The boards ranged from first to fourth on him, a spread of 3 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: he did not appear in an NBA game. Did not play — foot injury cost him his entire rookie season. 2025-26: 69 games, 69 starts, 28.9 minutes — 17.1 / 8.9 / 1.7 on 55.7% shooting. That ranked him second in the class and marked him at $808.24, +0.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $808.24, today's mark of $808.24 is +0.0%. The board reads that as MARKET GOT IT RIGHT — he is priced about where the consensus put him. He made All-NBA Third Team in 2025-26 with 87 vote points, which is why he sits where he does at that mark.

The rookie line on this page is blank because he never played it. A Lisfranc injury to his right foot, suffered in a Seattle pro-am in August 2022, cost him the entire 2022-23 season. He has been hurt twice more since: a right iliac wing fracture on Nov. 10, 2024, when he contested an Andrew Wiggins layup and landed hard, held to an eight-to-ten week window that became three months and 32 appearances; and a low back sprain in early November 2025.

None of it has cost him the job. Oklahoma City won the 2025 title, then paid him a five-year extension worth close to $240 million reported on July 9, 2025 — the third of the champion's major extensions after Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Jalen Williams. He played 69 games in 2025-26 at power forward alongside Isaiah Hartenstein, made All-Defensive First Team, and was one of three Defensive Player of the Year finalists with Victor Wembanyama and Ausar Thompson. The season ended in a Game 7 the champions lost, 111-103 to San Antonio, with Holmgren held to four points in 33 minutes.
