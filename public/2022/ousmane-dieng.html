SCOUTING REPORT

New York took him eleventh overall out of New Zealand Breakers in the Australian NBL. He had just gone for 8.9 points, 3.1 rebounds and 1.0 assists in 23 games at 20.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #14, pick 11. The boards ranged from twelfth to #20 on him, a spread of 8 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 39 games, 1 starts, 14.6 minutes — 4.9 / 2.7 / 1.2 on 42.0% shooting. That ranked him #24 in the class and marked him at $39.70, -61.9% against his IPO. 2025-26: 57 games, 20 starts, 19.3 minutes — 7.6 / 3.2 / 2.4 on 42.2% shooting. That ranked him #24 in the class and marked him at $39.70, -61.9% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $104.27, today's mark of $39.70 is -61.9%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

Draft night, June 23, 2022. New York made the pick at 11 and traded it to Oklahoma City. New York's return was three protected 2023 first-round picks — Detroit's, Denver's and Washington's, all via Oklahoma City. Dieng never became a rotation player there: 136 regular-season games over three and a half seasons at 12.0 minutes a night, 4.2 and 2.1, with a fractured right ring finger picked up playing for the Oklahoma City Blue in November 2024.

Then he changed hands three times in about 24 hours at the 2026 deadline. On Feb. 5 Oklahoma City sent him to Charlotte for Mason Plumlee. Hours later Charlotte packaged him with Collin Sexton and second-round picks to Chicago for Coby White and Mike Conley. On Feb. 6 a three-team deal moved him from Chicago to Milwaukee, with Cole Anthony and Amir Coffey going from Milwaukee to Phoenix and Nick Richards going from Phoenix to Chicago.

Milwaukee is where it worked: 11.0 / 4.6 / 3.6 in 30 games, and a three-year, $17.5 million re-signing announced July 9, 2026, running through 2028-29 with a team option on the final year.
