SCOUTING REPORT

Denver took him #21 overall out of Kansas. He had just gone for 14.1 points, 6.5 rebounds and 2.8 assists in 40 games at 34.3 minutes a night. He went 13 picks earlier than the boards had him — composite #34, drafted 21. Somebody in that room saw it differently. The boards ranged from #30 to #58 on him, a spread of 28 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 76 games, 6 starts, 15.5 minutes — 4.7 / 2.4 / 0.8 on 49.5% shooting. That ranked him #14 in the class and marked him at $104.27, +545.8% against his IPO. 2025-26: 44 games, 44 starts, 31.8 minutes — 12.0 / 4.8 / 2.7 on 51.9% shooting. That ranked him #18 in the class and marked him at $68.95, +327.1% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $16.15, today's mark of $68.95 is +327.1%. The board reads that as MARKET UNDERVALUED HIM — he is worth more now than the pre-draft consensus was willing to pay. He finished #14 in the All-Rookie vote among 2022 draftees.

He has never been traded, and he won an NBA championship in his rookie season. Denver extended him on Oct. 20, 2025 — five years, $125 million — and the season that followed could hardly have been timed less kindly. A left ankle sprain in a 130-116 win over the Clippers carried a six-week re-evaluation and became nearly two months; he appeared in three games between mid-November and early February and finished with 44 of 82.

What he played was uneven: 12.0 / 4.8 / 2.7 on 51.9% from the field but 30.1% from three, then 8.3 / 3.5 / 1.7 in a first-round loss he played through an injury to finish. He was blunt about it afterwards — that he had not played well enough individually and had not had the team ready for a tough series. Denver explored moving him and Cam Johnson for flexibility in the summer and did not, and with Peyton Watson traded to Cleveland he is described as cemented in the starting lineup for 2026-27.
