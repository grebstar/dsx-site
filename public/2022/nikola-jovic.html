Miami took him #27 overall out of Mega Basket in the ABA League. He had just gone for 11.7 points, 4.4 rebounds and 3.4 assists in 25 games at 27.8 minutes a night. The boards had him 10 places above where he went, at #17. The boards ranged from eleventh to #27 on him, a spread of 16 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 15 games, 8 starts, 13.6 minutes — 5.5 / 2.1 / 0.7 on 40.6% shooting. That ranked him #30 in the class and marked him at $23.39, -69.2% against his IPO. 2025-26: 47 games, 1 starts, 17.2 minutes — 7.3 / 3.3 / 2.2 on 36.6% shooting. That ranked him #25 in the class and marked him at $36.38, -52.1% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $75.99, today's mark of $36.38 is -52.1%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player.

He signed a four-year, $62.4 million extension, official Oct. 10, 2025, which took him off the restricted free agency track before the deadline — and then posted career lows across the board inside it. He played 47 games with career lows across the board, shooting 26.9% from three on 3.6 attempts against 37.0% on 3.8 attempts over his first three seasons. One number went the other way: he gave up 0.82 points per possession in isolation, the second-best mark on the team.

The absences were a broken second metacarpal in his right hand in February 2025, a right elbow injury after a mid-air collision against Toronto on Dec. 15, 2025 that left him in an air cast, and a lower back problem that cost 11 games before he returned on Mar. 22, 2026. He was not part of the July 6, 2026 trade in which Miami sent Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware, Kasparas Jakucionis, the 13th pick and a stack of future firsts to Milwaukee for Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis — but he has been named in reporting since as a player Miami could move to acquire shooting, at $16.2 million in 2026-27 and under a poison-pill restriction. He scored 21 for Serbia against Iceland on Aug. 31, 2026.
