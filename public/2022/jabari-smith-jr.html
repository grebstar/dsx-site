SCOUTING REPORT

Houston took him third overall out of Auburn. He had just gone for 16.9 points, 7.4 rebounds and 2.0 assists in 34 games at 28.8 minutes a night. The boards and the draft room agreed almost exactly: composite #1, pick 3. The boards ranged from first to third on him, a spread of 2 places across 5 of them.

2022-23: 79 games, 79 starts, 31.0 minutes — 12.8 / 7.2 / 1.3 on 40.8% shooting. That ranked him eighth in the class and marked him at $229.10, -77.1% against his IPO. 2025-26: 77 games, 77 starts, 35.1 minutes — 15.8 / 6.9 / 1.9 on 45.0% shooting. That ranked him seventh in the class and marked him at $269.91, -73.0% against his IPO.

Against a frozen IPO of $1,000.00, today's mark of $269.91 is -73.0%. The board reads that as MARKET OVERVALUED HIM. That is a statement about the pre-draft price, not a verdict on the player. He finished eighth in the All-Rookie vote among 2022 draftees.

He has never been traded and he has never lost the starting job. Houston signed him to a five-year, $122 million extension on July 6, 2025, fully guaranteed, beginning in 2026-27 at $23,643,411 and running through 2030-31, with a poison-pill trade restriction that ran to July 1, 2026. On Oct. 4, 2025 Ime Udoka named him to the opening-night five alongside Kevin Durant, Amen Thompson and Alperen Sengun.

The one real absence was a broken left hand, a metacarpal broken at a shootaround on Jan. 3, 2025, which cost him 22 games; Udoka brought him back off the bench on Feb. 21 despite his having started every game before it. Houston finished fifth in the West in 2025-26 and lost a first-round series to the Lakers, Smith playing 42 and 43 minutes a night in it and closing Game 6 with nine points on 3-of-11 and 12 rebounds in a 98-78 defeat.
